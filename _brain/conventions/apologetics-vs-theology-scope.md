# Apologetics vs theology scope

Decided by Tom on 2026-09-24, founding the implant
([journal](../journals/archive/2026/09/2026-09-24.md)):

> *"Apologetics is basically everything that defends Christian beliefs… bluntly
> put, it is articles about subjects that help Christians defend their faith
> against non-Christian views. And theology is about articles that concern
> subjects that help Christians defend their Christian views against other
> Christian views."*

This is the scope rule that separates this implant from its sibling,
`brain-implant-theology`. Both are brainpick brains; both inherit the same
epistemic conventions; they differ in *who the argument is addressed to*.

## The rule

**Apologetics defends the faith against outsiders; theology defends a reading
of the faith against other readers of the faith.** The boundary is not the
subject — both implants discuss the resurrection, the Bible, the existence of
God — but the *opponent* the article's argument engages with.

| | apologetics | theology |
|---|---|---|
| opponent | non-Christian views | other Christian views |
| example | a defense of the historical Jesus against skeptical claims | a defense of Calvinist soteriology against Arminian soteriology |
| this implant | ✅ in scope | ❌ out of scope — belongs in the theology implant |

An article that engages both is placed where its *primary* argument lands,
and cross-linked to the other implant with a `brain://` pointer. An article
that neither engages non-Christian views nor intra-Christian debate is out of
scope for both implants.

## Neutral point of view — the rule both implants share

**We claim that somebody *said* something. We do not claim that what they
said is true.** The statement "Augustine argued that evil is the privation of
good" is a claim about what Augustine said — it is true or false, and it can
be checked against Augustine's published text. The statement "evil is the
privation of good" is a theological claim — it may be true or false, and
*this implant does not take a position on it.*

This is not evasion; it is the only honest epistemics for a repository that
spans traditions that disagree. An implant that asserted "evil is the
privation of good" as fact would be an Augustinian implant, not an
apologetics implant. The value this implant adds — like
[Every claim carries its receipt](every-claim-carries-its-receipt.md) — is
not the claim but the source: who said it, where, and in what words.

### What this means in practice

1. **Attribute, don't assert.** "Plantinga argues that properly basic belief
   in God can be rational without evidence ([source])" — not "belief in God
   is rational without evidence." The first is checkable; the second is a
   position.
2. **Present all theist viewpoints neutrally.** ApoWiki's founding principle
   — "esitellä kaikki kristillis-teistiset näkökulmat neutraalissa
   valossa älyllisen rehellisyyden nimissä" (present all Christian-theist
   viewpoints in a neutral light, in the name of intellectual honesty) — is
   inherited verbatim. A Reformed argument and a Catholic argument for the
   same conclusion are both presented, both cited, neither favored.
3. **Criticism lands on the argument, not the person.** See
   [Evidence before authority](evidence-before-authority.md). "This argument
   assumes a premise the opponent would not grant ([source])" is in scope;
   "this apologist is a crank" is not.
4. **The truth of the defended claim is out of scope.** This implant defends
   the *availability* of the argument, not its correctness. A reader who
   disagrees changes the inputs and recomputes — the
   [credence model](credence-is-a-model-not-a-measurement.md) is for exactly
   that.

## The Bible is shared

Both implants reference the biblical text. The theology implant bootstraps
with Bible translations and commentaries; this implant references verses and
chapters as they appear in apologetic arguments. The Bible itself is not
apologetics or theology — it is the shared source text both argue about, and
it lives in whichever implant ingests it first, cross-linked from the other.

## Why this is a convention and not a task

Scope creep is the silent failure of a knowledge repository. An apologetics
implant that drifts into theology becomes two half-implants; a theology
implant that drifts into apologetics loses its focus on intra-Christian
debate. The boundary is decided once, written here, and applied to every
future page — including pages written by agents and contributors who never
read this conversation. It is `type: convention` with `half_life: 0` so it
never fades from `brain_overview`.
