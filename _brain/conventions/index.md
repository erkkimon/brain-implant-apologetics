# Conventions

Standing rules for how work is done here — naming, process, contracts the
brain's keepers hold themselves to. One kebab-case page per rule,
`type: convention`, listed here. Not a specific piece of work (that is
`plans/`), not a step-by-step procedure (that is `skills/`), not the record
of choosing (that is a `decision`) — the standing answer, applied broadly.

## Conventions

Read these first — a rule constrains what every other read is for.

* [Written to be public](written-to-be-public.md) — every page is addressed to a stranger on the open internet: no host names, no internal paths, no personal detail, no credentials, no bare IP addresses. The repository is private only until it is published; that changes nothing about how it is written.
* [Every claim carries its receipt](every-claim-carries-its-receipt.md) — no statement of fact without a resolvable citation attached to that statement; the fact is cheap, the source is the product. Derivations, assumptions and judgements are allowed and labelled; confident unsourced sentences are not.
* [Evidence before authority](evidence-before-authority.md) — claims are graded by their evidence, never by who made them; criticism lands on the argument; and "is this really their claim?" (provenance) is a separate question from "is it true?" (feasibility).
* [Credence is a model, not a measurement](credence-is-a-model-not-a-measurement.md) — a probability here is the output of a stated, replayable model (decomposition, prior with its reference class, likelihood ratios with citations), never a claim to have measured a truth; readers substitute their own inputs and recompute, and prior-sensitivity is what separates evidence from faith.
* [Apologetics vs theology scope](apologetics-vs-theology-scope.md) — apologetics defends Christian belief against non-Christian views; theology defends one Christian view against other Christian views; the boundary is "who is the opponent," and both implants hold the same neutral-point-of-view rule: we claim that somebody *said* something (which is true and provable), not that what they said is true.
