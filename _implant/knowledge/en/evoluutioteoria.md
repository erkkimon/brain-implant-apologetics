# Theory of evolution

*Topics: [Theory of evolution](../topics/evoluutioteoria.md), [Incomplete articles](../topics/keskeneräiset-artikkelit.md).*


**On terminology** On ApoWiki, the theory of evolution means the doctrine according to which *"all organisms descend from a common ancestor through unguided mutations and natural selection."* This view is also called the synthetic theory of evolution or neo-Darwinism. There are also theories of evolution other than neo-Darwinism. On ApoWiki, however, the theory of evolution means neo-Darwinism unless otherwise stated. The meanings of the word evolution are discussed in the article [definition of evolution](evoluution-määritelmä.md).

The **theory of evolution** is a natural-historical view according to which all existing and past organisms have developed from a common ancestral form through unguided mutations and natural selection. Formulated this way, an essential part of the theory is the view that the origin and development of organisms is a purposeless process governed by the laws of nature. Ever since the father of the theory, **Charles Darwin**, published the book *On the Origin of Species* (1859), in which he set out the central principles of the theory, the theory of evolution has been the subject of heated debate. Today the theory of evolution represents the general view of the scientific community on the history of life. Yet from the theory's earliest days, the claim that the processes are unguided has been criticized. In recent decades, among other things, the focus of the debate has shifted to the question of whether mutations and natural selection can explain all biological and biochemical observations. In particular, the theory of evolution's assumption that God has no place whatsoever in the development of life, as Darwin categorically insisted, poses its own apologetic challenge to Christian theism.

According to the theory of evolution, life has developed through random and unguided processes. Neo-Darwinism thus contains the following component claims:
1. [Variation](perinnöllisen-muuntelun-mekanismit.md). The claim that variation and natural selection occur.
1. [Common descent](polveutumisoppi.md) (Engl. *common descent*). The claim that all living things have developed from a single organism.
1. Sufficiency. The claim that variation and natural selection have, over a long period of time, brought about all the complex structures around us and the functional information needed to produce them.
1. Unguidedness. The claim that variation is random, or at least that it is not guided by any person.

Only the 1st of these claims can be regarded as an indisputable scientific fact. Evidence based on empirical observations has also been presented for the 2nd claim, although this body of evidence is not entirely unambiguous. No actual body of evidence has been presented for the 3rd claim. The claim is often assumed to be a self-evident conclusion once the 1st and 2nd claims have been proven. Such thinking seems consistent from a materialist point of view, but from the point of view of Christian theism, for example, the claim is easily called into question. The fourth claim is by nature a purely philosophical, non-scientific assumption.[^1]

The theory of evolution can thus be seen as a combination (claim 3) of two mutually independent ideas (claims 1 and 2).
The first is the idea of variation, that is, that the genome of species can change over time, mainly through mutations and natural selection. The first idea is thus a fairly general statement. The genome of species surely does change somewhat over time. What kind and what size of variation can happen this way is quite another matter.
The second idea holds that all organisms are related to one another and that they descend from a common ancestral form.
The theory of evolution combines these two ideas so that variation is assumed to explain the changes that common descent requires. The theory of evolution thus assumes that the changes occurring in organisms would be a sufficient cause to explain the whole diversity of biology.

There is good evidence of small-scale variation, such as antibiotic resistance in bacteria or the variation in the beak sizes of finches. It does not follow from this that all organisms would have arisen through that same process. No evidence has really even been presented for this enormous extrapolation. On the contrary, there is much evidence[^2] indicating that large changes are not possible by this mechanism.

### The philosophy-of-science element

For the last 150 years, the fashion in the philosophy of science has been naturalism, according to which only natural explanations may be used in science. From this philosophical prior commitment it follows that explanatory models appealing to any purposeful course of events have not been admitted into scientific discussion, not even when dealing with the Earth's [biodiversity](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/biodiversiteetti), so some kind of purposeless process is the only scientifically acceptable explanation for the origin of organisms, whether or not the observations support that explanation.

With this restriction, the options that remain are in practice either (1) everything arising at once through pure chance, (2) some law of nature that inevitably produces complex organisms, or (3) some combination of the laws of nature and chance, in which chance produces complexity and law preserves the accumulating complexity. Of these, option (3) can be considered the least improbable, which in practice means some kind of natural descent, life developing by itself naturally and gradually.

This is precisely the basic idea of evolutionary models, and it may well look good on paper. Because of the aforementioned advance restriction of the space of scientific discussion, it has not mattered at all whether this basic idea can be supported or confirmed experimentally: since all research and discussion takes place within its framework, professing it is required at every stage of the research process as a condition for being accepted as a party to scientific discussion, and so it has not been possible to question it publicly without being penalized.[^3] For that reason science has also been unable to take note of the evidence presented for design, and this has happened entirely regardless of what kind of evidence is available or what kind of rational arguments for design have been presented. It is therefore highly problematic that the prevailing philosophy of science restricts the field of permitted answers in science this strongly.[^4]

### The worldview element

For the materialist and atheist worldviews, the theory of evolution is an almost indispensable article of faith, without which these positions cannot face observable reality. That is why materialists hold on to the theory regardless of the empirical evidence; after all, they could not question it without at the same time shaking their materialist or atheist [properly basic belief](../glossary/sanasto.md#perususkomus).

### History of the theory of evolution

* Main article: [History of the theory of evolution](evoluutioteorian-historia.md)

Evolutionary ideas were already put forward in ancient Greece by **Lucretius** and other materialists, whose views required some kind of spontaneous generation of life from matter. Thus Lucretius proposed that in swamps the heads, legs and arms of creatures arise entirely by themselves, and that sometimes they just happen to come together suitably, forming a viable whole, which then sets off to plod around its surroundings.

Before **[Blaise Pascal](../articles/blaise-pascal.md)**'s sterile experiments, it was commonly believed that, for example, rats arise by themselves if one leaves a sack of grain lying at the back of a room. Speculations about the spontaneous origin of life are still common today, and they are plagued by the same lack of evidence and by the community's unwillingness to subject its speculations to hard and public self-criticism.

Charles Darwin, who presented his theory of development, known as Darwinism, in 1859 in his book *On the Origin of Species*, is generally regarded as the father of the theory of evolution. According to Darwin's idea, the changes in biodiversity that common descent requires would have arisen over the ages through small heritable variation and natural selection.

### Empirical evidence

#### Predictions of the theory of evolution

To the extent that evolution is a random process and evolution has no direction, the theory of evolution predicts nothing well. On the other hand, since precise knowledge of the viability of different solutions is usually lacking, stories can be invented quite freely, and observations are often easy to explain after the fact. Because the theory of evolution predicts everything, it predicts nothing well. Although many scientific discoveries are said to have been made under the guidance of the theory of evolution, the researchers who made these breakthroughs have not considered the theory of evolution a significant influence, but believe they would have done the same experiments without it.[^5]

According to the ideals of science, models should be tested again and again against new observations, whereby the original observations are either confirmed or overturned. Contrary to the ideals of science, however, the theory of evolution itself is hardly ever questioned. Most researchers are willing to question only how the theory of evolution works, not whether the doctrine of unguided development is true at all. In other words, they are not willing to question Darwinism's most essential claim, that unguided mutations and natural selection are able to produce the enormous complexity of the entire living world. Past evolution cannot be studied experimentally, but present evolution can, and with the help of indirect evidence, the credibility of the theory of evolution's claims about history can be assessed. The population sizes and numbers of generations of viruses, parasites, bacteria and fruit flies are enormously larger than those of, for example, mammal species. Experiments done with these show that random mutations and natural selection can produce only [very minor results](the-edge-of-evolution-kirja.md), not the large structural changes that life requires.

##### A Cambrian hare?

It is often claimed that if, for example, a rabbit were found in the oldest rock strata, the theory of evolution would be proven false. Such a find, if it happened, would falsify common descent, and since that is a necessary part of the theory of evolution, it would also falsify the theory of evolution. This would, however, be a poor falsification criterion, because it would at the same time also falsify guided common descent, which is one of the significant alternatives to the theory of evolution. A falsification criterion should therefore be such that it would be enough to refute the theory in question but would not refute its competitors. Otherwise this criterion could be used to falsify only the claim that rabbits arose later than these old strata. In practice this criterion makes young-earth creationism the [null hypothesis](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nollahypoteesi) of the theory of evolution, and implies that the Darwinian theory of evolution is the best explanation as long as the young-earth theory finds no support. Claiming that a Cambrian rabbit would be a suitable falsification criterion for the Darwinian theory of evolution actually commits a [false dichotomy](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Väärä_dilemma) <!-- red link: Väärä vastakkainasettelu -->, and so the criterion is not really valid.

It is not, however, even entirely clear that such a find would falsify common descent, since fossils found in the "wrong" stratum have traditionally been explained with various [ad hoc hypotheses](../glossary/sanasto.md#ad-hoc-hypoteesi). If rabbits had been found in lower strata, the assumed age of that stratum would be questioned, because it would be "known" that rabbits did not live in such old strata. Then the radiometric measurements would be declared faulty because of contamination, and recourse would be had to the index fossil method. In other words, the age of the stratum would be determined from the rabbit, not the age of the rabbit from the stratum. Alternatively, mammals could be redefined as an earlier group of organisms than previously thought, their place in the assumed tree of life would be closer to the root, and the find would no longer cause problems for the theory.

##### Continuity of the fossil record

It can also be said that the theory of evolution would predict a continuous fossil record, whereas design would predict a discontinuous one. The fossil record is discontinuous,[^6][^7] and for this reason [paleontologists](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/paleontologi) introduced punctualism, that is, the model of punctuated equilibrium, into the theory of evolution. According to punctualism, populations remain unchanged for long periods, and changes happen quickly in small populations whose fossilization is unlikely. It is generally rare to find an unbroken series of fossils showing an ancestral species gradually changing into another species. Only a fraction of all the organisms that have lived on Earth have been fossilized. Many strata lay in areas that were washed away or destroyed as the continental plates moved. Strata were folded, flattened or deformed, destroying the fossil record; moreover, only a fraction of the fossil-bearing strata reach the surface today. The record is considered extensive enough to establish that its general features are sudden appearance and the stability of species. This does not match the original prediction of the theory of evolution. Several false predictions have been made on the basis of the theory of evolution, but the theory's predictions are sufficiently adjustable, so the false predictions are not seen as problematic.[^8]

##### A complex structure that small changes cannot explain?

It can, however, be said that the theory of evolution gives a low prediction for structures that require several changes before the structure confers a selective advantage. This principle is used within the theory of evolution when considering which developmental pathway would be the most plausible, and it ought to be usable when considering whether the theory of evolution itself is plausible. Design gives a high prediction for multi-part systems, because designers foresee which parts a particular function needs. Charles Darwin put forward this criterion in 1859 in his book On the Origin of Species:

> If it could be shown that there is even one organ that could not have developed through numerous small beneficial changes, my theory would collapse completely.
>
> — Charles Darwin, On the Origin of Species (1859)[^9]

It is, however, practically impossible to show that the origin of some structure would be theoretically entirely impossible, at least if one is willing to accept arbitrarily improbable events. Rather than trying to show that the origin of some structure through the Darwinian mechanism is logically impossible, it is appropriate to examine whether the origin of that structure through the Darwinian mechanism is biologically reasonable. The lack of such explanations is, however, nowadays not regarded as a problem for the theory, but only for whoever questions the theory: even if you do not understand how a structure could have developed, that does not mean it did not develop.

### The status of the theory of evolution in the scientific community

The great majority of scientists regard the theory of evolution as a good natural-scientific model. On the other hand, it can be said that most of them have not acquainted themselves with the material critical of the theory.[^10] The theory of evolution has a monopoly position in biology, and criticism of it is hardly tolerated in the academic environment. The situation is unusually unscientific.

In biology, as in science in general, nothing is proven with complete certainty. Yet many defenders of the theory of evolution behave as if the theory had been proven absolutely. In fact, there is surprisingly little evidence that mutation and natural selection could bring about the claimed and essential large changes, for example new structures or new biochemical systems.

### Evidence for the theory of evolution

#### Evidence for heritable variation

* Main article: [Mechanisms of heritable variation](perinnöllisen-muuntelun-mekanismit.md)

Things that testify to limited variation include, among others, mutations arising in the genomes of organisms, observations of speciation, beneficial mutations becoming common or disappearing, and the rapid appearance of resistance in bacterial populations. These phenomena are not, however, discussed in this article, because these observations speak only of variation and do not prove neo-Darwinism's main claim about how organisms came to be.

#### Evidence for common descent

* Main article: [Common descent](polveutumisoppi.md)

Regarded as evidence for common descent are, among other things, the coding system based on DNA shared by organisms, the genetic code claimed to be universal, structural and genetic homologies, vestigial organs, the consistent geological layering of the fossil record and its structural development. This evidence is discussed in its own article.

#### Evidence for the sufficiency of the mechanisms of variation

According to the theory of evolution, the mechanisms of variation are sufficient to explain the origin of biodiversity. This is the core claim of the theory of evolution, and evidence ought to be offered for this claim for the theory of evolution to be considered credible.

#### Conclusions on the evidence for the theory of evolution

In popular contexts, the theory of evolution is presented as an absolutely certain fact that all our observations in biology support. In reality, the evidence presented for the theory of evolution has been strongly questioned, or it testifies only to small-scale variation. Within the scientific community there is much critical discussion about several areas of the theory of evolution, and a number of natural-scientific objections to it can indeed be found. In public, however, researchers can rarely afford to be dissenters. When researchers compete for funding, reputation and posts, the threshold for publicly admitting doubts about the validity of Darwin's theory rises. The book *[Evoluutio – kriittinen analyysi](../articles/evoluutio-kriittinen-analyysi-kirja.md)* aptly states

> The origin and history of life are unique events of the past. It is evident that worldview positions influence the research concerning them. In order to assess the significance of different models of origins, it is necessary to distinguish between objective results, theoretical interpretations and worldview presuppositions.[^10]

### Problems with the theory of evolution

Below are presented some pieces of evidence that make believing in the theory of evolution difficult, unless one is already committed to the truth of the theory, either through a commitment to natural causes or through an unscientific faith in the authority of the scientific community. Evidence against the theory of evolution is also discussed on the page [Arguments against the theory of evolution](../articles/evoluutioteorian-vastaiset-argumentit.md).

#### The edge of evolution

Because a philosophy of science committed to natural causes and the atheist worldview have demanded some natural explanation for the living world, the theory of evolution has been assumed to be capable of producing the incomprehensibly complex structures observed in the living world. So the matter has precisely only been assumed, and it has not been considered necessary even to try to prove the claim empirically.

In the book [The Edge of Evolution](the-edge-of-evolution-kirja.md), biochemistry professor **[Michael Behe](../articles/michael-behe.md)** examines the observed mutations and natural selection in malaria, in HIV and in the genome of a local human population. Because in these cases the selective advantages are especially large, and the HIV and malaria parasite populations are large and mutate rapidly, this is the best available evidence for the ability of mutation and natural selection and for its limits. So let us look at direct observations of the theory of evolution's ability to produce new solutions for organisms. On the basis of observational measurements, it can be concluded that nothing essentially new arises.<!-- Ilman epärealistista oletusta huomattavista valintaeduista jokaiselle välivaiheelle jokaisen mutaation jälkeen, parhaimmillaan evoluution voidaan ajatella tuottavan kaksi tiettyä mutaatiota. !Behe ei väitä tätä!--> Evolution therefore has clear limits, and it is not able to produce essentially new information, new biochemical systems or new structures.[^11]

#### The impossibility of protein evolution

**[Douglas Axe](http://biologicinstitute.org/people/)** has carried out experimental research according to which, already among chains of 150 amino acids, only one in 10<sup>62</sup> can fold into a stable, biologically functional form[^12] [^13]. The chance probability of achieving a particular function is about 10<sup>-77</sup>. With the Earth's resources (10<sup>50</sup> atoms, at most 10<sup>18</sup> seconds, and on the other hand at most only 10<sup>40</sup> cells over the history of the Earth), therefore, not even a single functional protein can be produced purely by chance. Most cell functions require at least six working proteins, so at this stage of the analysis the chance probabilities are already astronomically small. The theory of evolution requires as its starting point a first cell that arose randomly and without purpose. Yet even the chance origin of the sufficiently long amino acid chains such a cell requires is [impossible](../articles/abiogeneesi.md), and this look at protein functionality shows that the matter also involves enormous additional problems.

Looking at the set of possible amino acid chains as a "map", then, it turns out that between the "islands" of functional proteins there is a vast "sea" of non-function. In principle it would still be conceivable that between two functional islands there is a long developmental pathway of functional proteins, each reachable from the next by small single changes, which would moreover confer a selective advantage along its whole length. Is it then credible that between two islands this distant[^14] there really would be a path of harmless intermediate steps?[^15] Precisely the empirical evidence that is fundamentally important to the natural sciences shows that the path across the "sea of non-function" that the theory of evolution requires does not exist even in easy cases[^16].[^17]

#### Convergences

[Convergence](konvergenssi.md) means similarity that common descent does not explain. For example, eyes and wings would have had to develop many times independently of each other. There are thus many similarities that common descent does not explain, in which case, according to the theory of evolution, they would have had to develop as organisms adapted to a shared ecological niche. Consequently, the similarities that common descent would explain are in fact merely the result of selecting the evidence.

#### Discontinuity and stability of the fossil record

The best examples of intermediate forms and developmental series are presented from time to time in support of the theory of evolution. In reality, even from these examples only those features are used that can be considered to have developed as a series. Features that fit the series poorly are often left unmentioned, and often the constructed series does not agree with the dating either.
The most essential feature of the fossil record, however, is its discontinuity and stability: new structures appear suddenly and the solutions are very stable after their appearance, with only small variation taking place. This fits the prediction of design theory. The direct prediction of the theory of evolution is gradually progressing change, which is thus not observed at the macro level in the fossil record.[^6][^20]

#### The slowness of the evolutionary mechanism

Evolution should proceed one small mutation at a time. Because even beneficial mutations typically give only a small advantage in a largely random struggle for survival, the accumulation of even beneficial mutations in a population is slow. It is especially slow if an intermediate step is even slightly harmful. For example, the accumulation of two particular mutations in the human genome takes 100 million years if the first mutation causes a 5% disadvantage, which is a generous assumption for evolution. This pace is simply not enough.[^21]

#### Optimality of the DNA code

It has been shown that the coding system of DNA is very [close to optimal](kosminen-hienosäätö.md).
According to the theory of evolution, this would be mere chance, because it is practically impossible for the code to change by small steps, since the meaning of all the encoded information would change at every step. According to naturalism, the coding system of DNA is thus a "frozen accident".

#### The number of harmful mutations

According to John C. Sanford, empirical observations point to the degeneration of organisms' genomes.[^22] Unfortunately, natural selection is not able even to preserve the genome, as Sanford shows in his book. Sanford's argument has many sides; most of the results below are fatal to the theory of evolution even on their own:
1. Most mutations are nearly neutral, that is, they are so slightly harmful that natural selection cannot weed them out because of normal noise and genetic drift. Such harmful mutations accumulate in the genome generation after generation.
1. Fewer than one mutation in a million is favorable in terms of selection pressure, and even these are usually based on the destruction of information. For example, antibiotic resistance can be achieved when the part of the bacterium's metabolic machinery that the antibiotic targets breaks. (Your computer gets fewer viruses when the internet connection is broken.) In addition, because of genetic drift alone, fewer than one in a thousand beneficial mutations come to prevail in a population.
1. The mutation rate in humans is 200–6000 mutations per person per generation. Population-genetic calculations show that natural selection can weed out at most 0.5 mutations per person per generation. The human genome is therefore degenerating, and natural selection cannot keep up with weeding out the errors.
1. Mitochondrial mutations pose a similar problem.
1. Organisms reproduce and survive largely for reasons other than genetic advantages. Nutrition, upbringing and chance affect survival and reproduction far more than small genetic advantages do. Natural selection is thus like a princess who sleeps on top of many mattresses and tries to feel the peas, that is, the genetic errors, under them. For this reason natural selection is much less effective than is usually assumed.
1. When one tries to select for one favorable mutation, several harmful mutations have to be carried along with it (Muller’s ratchet).
