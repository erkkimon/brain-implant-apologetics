# The Bible

*Topics: [The Bible](../topics/raamattu.md).*

**The Bible** ([Gk.](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/kreikan_kieli) *grammata*, 'writings') is the foundational book of [Christian theism](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/kristillinen_teismi). Judging by how it came into being, it can be regarded as a collection of books rather than a single book, but in its content it forms an astonishingly unified, coherent whole. All Christians, and many non-Christians too, believe it to be (in one way or another) God's revelation.

### The textual history of the Bible

When aiming for conceptual precision about the Bible, it is worth distinguishing between
1. the original manuscripts of the Bible,
1. the manuscript finds of the Bible,
1. the reconstruction of the Bible in its original languages, and
1. the translations of the Bible.

#### Original manuscripts and manuscript finds

Like every text ever written, the books of the Bible too had original manuscripts. As far as is known, however, these have not survived to our time; instead, our knowledge of the text of the Bible rests on thousands of manuscripts preserved from ancient times, each of which contains handwritten Bible text. Some of these finds consist of short passages, i.e. fragments, some contain one book of the Bible, and some several books together. These manuscripts came about by copying the original manuscripts or their direct or indirect copies; they are thus like witnesses, each of which tells as best it can what the original manuscripts of the Bible contained.

#### Biblical textual criticism and the reconstructed text

Biblical textual criticism means the work of research and reconstruction (rebuilding) whose aim is to obtain the most reliable possible picture of what the original manuscripts of the books of the Bible were like. Its starting point is all known manuscript finds, and by combining them and selecting among them on good grounds, it aims in principle to get as close to the original forms of the text as possible. This research has produced scholarly critical editions of the Bible which, besides the reconstructed original-language text, contain among other things information on textual variants, i.e. small differences in the details of the manuscript finds, and references to the finds that attest each variant. This additional information is usually gathered at the foot of each page of reconstructed text in what is called the *apparatus*.

#### Bible translations and church use

When translating the Bible, one must first choose the form of the text on which the translation will be based. Often the scholarly reconstructed text of the latest edition of some critical edition is followed, but besides these there are also certain classical forms of the text, long known by name[^1], which can be taken as the basis of a translation as they stand if so desired.

Despite differences in small details, such as word order, these choices of base text do not, taken as a whole, appreciably affect the end result of translation projects – the Bible says essentially the same thing in all of its manuscripts that have been found.

So, for example, the doctrinal disputes between denominations do not arise because the Bible translations they accept used different forms of the original-language text, but because the denominations, for historical reasons if nothing else, represent different overall theological systems of reasoning, which are based on the Bible only in part.[^2]

### The history of the word "Bible" and the boundaries of the Bible

#### The Bible then and now

Because the books of the Bible come from different periods, the word Bible itself has meant collections of books of different sizes at different times:[^3] no book could belong to the Bible, i.e. to the holy scriptures, before it was written, that is, before it belonged to any writings at all.

So, for example, the Bible Jesus used corresponds[^4] to the present Old Testament. The New Testament, in turn, is a collection of the earliest Christian writings. The whole formed by the Old and New Testaments together is known today as the Bible.

#### Delimiting the Bible acknowledged as the Word of God

Which of the existing writings are, in the end, to be regarded as holy texts, i.e. as the authoritative Word of God? The answer to this question is, at its shortest, a kind of table of contents of the Bible: the list of the names of the books belonging to the Bible, that is, the *[canon](../articles/kaanon.md)*. Books belonging to such a canon are therefore called *canonical books*, and the question of what the list contains is called the *canon question*.

The content of the New Testament canon is in its main features the same in the lists of authoritative books given by all early Christian writers.[^5] The text of every Bible printed as a book always corresponds to some canon.

Because the answers given to the canon question differ in part, Roman Catholics and Protestants, for example, have slightly different Bibles – on the basis of a decision of the Council of Trent, held in the 16th century, the Old Testament of the Roman church has about seven more books than that of the Evangelical Lutherans.[^6]

### Interpretation of the Bible, its influence, and Bible translation work

#### Interpretations are not of equal value

It has become a kind of truism or even a mantra that "the Bible can be interpreted in many ways".[^7] All texts can of course be interpreted in many ways, but it does not follow that all the interpretations put forward are in any way of equal value. In seeking the correct interpretation of the Bible, as of other texts, each of us should therefore use and develop our [argumentation-analysis](../articles/argumentaatioanalyysi.md) skills as best we can.

The cultural and historical influence of the Bible has undeniably been significant. The need to put the message of the Bible into a form the common people could understand was, for example, a decisive factor in the birth of written Finnish.

#### The current state of Bible translation worldwide

<!--Nykyäänkin Raamattua aletaan kääntää uudelle kielelle n. joka viides päivä,[^11]-->

Today Bible translation work often begins with building the written language in question.[^12] So that the people can benefit from the translated Bible, they are also taught to read, and in this way promoting literacy goes hand in hand with translating the Bible.[^12]

According to figures published in October 2016, there are about 7,097 languages in the world, and the whole Bible has been translated into 636 of them. In addition, the whole New Testament has been translated into 1,442 languages, and at least part of the Bible into 1,145 languages. In total, then, Bible scriptures are available in 3,223 languages. Most of the world's population, about 5.1 billion people, speak as their mother tongue a language into which the whole Bible has been translated. More than 685 million more belong to language groups that have at least the whole New Testament. There are nevertheless still about 253 million people in the world, belonging to 3,874 language groups, for whose mother tongue no Bible translation work is known to have begun.[^13] The Bible is also clearly the best-selling book in the world.[^14][^15]
