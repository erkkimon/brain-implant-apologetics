# Definition of religion

*Topics: [Worldview](../topics/maailmankatsomus.md).*

Religion has been defined in many ways, because defining it is extremely difficult. According to Outi Pohjanheimo, there is no consensus on the definition of religion.[^1] The question has come up especially in discussions of whether [atheism](ateismi.md) is a religion. Like the word [evolution](../articles/evoluutio-täsmennyssivu.md), the word religion has several definitions, which vary from one speaker and writer to another and often also by field.

### Definitions of religion
Definitions of religion most often founder either because (1) they do not cover all the belief systems regarded as religions, or because (2) the definition takes in a large number of belief systems that are not generally regarded as religions.

#### Cognitive definition
In philosophical discussions and in discussions comparing worldviews, the word religion is defined broadly and even-handedly: a religion is a faith-based view that takes a stand on the ultimate questions of reality. Professors T. Puolimatka[^2] and R. Clouser, for example, define religion in this way. By this definition, [theism](teismi.md), [atheism](ateismi.md), [dualism](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dualismi_(filosofia)) and [naturalism](naturalismi.md) are all religions. This definition should be used when discussing the credibility and truth of Christianity, atheism or other religions, because it brings out, even-handedly, the deep faith-based assumptions about reality that every position makes, assumptions that adherents of more superficial views seldom even notice themselves. No position then looks more credible merely by superficially avoiding the label of religion. Moreover, the practices tied to the various positions, which other definitions link to religion, have little bearing on credibility and truth.

#### Functional definition
By this definition, religion involves practices characteristic of it, such as regular gatherings, certain repeated actions ([rituals](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/rituaali)) or proclamation. This definition is partly problematic, because not even all the [major religions](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/valtauskonnot) necessarily have such characteristic practices. On the other hand, many organizations of [New Atheists](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/uusateismi) or freethinkers meet regularly, proselytize ([R. Dawkins's](richard-dawkins.md) book [The God Delusion](jumalharha-kirja.md), the London bus advertising campaign, the magazine *Vapaa Ajattelija* [The Freethinker]), run summer camps for children,[^3][^4] and their members may display emblems typical of their community, for example on T-shirts and cars.

#### Definition as pleasing God by works
In this definition, religion is seen as a worldview that includes actions by which a person tries to become acceptable to God or the gods.[^5] By this definition Christianity is not a religion, because in Christianity salvation comes by grace through faith in Jesus and his atoning work. According to Christianity, a human being is too wicked to enter into fellowship with the holy God, let alone to save himself, so Jesus came to earth to live a life acceptable to God and to suffer on the cross the punishment that people deserve. Jesus' life is credited to a person if that person relies on Him in this matter.

#### Definition by reference to the major religions
This definition is common but hard to justify. Religions vary in how widespread they are, and it is hard to justify why only the most widespread positions should be called religions.

#### Definition by reference to irrationality
Some people who are unfamiliar with the discussion in the philosophy of religion, or who have met only fideistic religiosity, define religion as a position that has no justification (a [fideistic](../glossary/sanasto.md#fideismi) view of religion). In reality, many religions argue for their position. In the case of Christianity, [Jesus](jeesus-nasaretilainen.md) himself, the [apostles](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/apostolit) and later the [Church Fathers](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/kirkkoisät) (especially [Augustine](../articles/augustinus.md), [Thomas Aquinas](tuomas-akvinolainen.md) and [Anselm](../articles/anselm-canterburylainen.md)) have [argued for](../articles/kristinuskon-perustelut.md) the truth of Christianity. In fact, the fideistic view of religion is a very recent one.<!--lähde?--> Moreover, those who hold this definition often fail to notice their own faith-based beliefs. For example, using reason and being rational presuppose a faith-based commitment to [the reliability of reason](../articles/älyllisten-kykyjen-luotettavuus.md), which according to [Alvin Plantinga](../articles/alvin-plantinga.md), among others, [cannot be justified within non-Christian worldviews](../articles/alvin-plantinga-evoluutioteoreettinen-argumentti-naturalismia-vastaan.md).

#### Theistic definition
Definitions based on theism define religion as belief in [gods](jumala.md).

The problem with this definition can be seen in the fact that some belief systems that are firmly regarded as religions, such as [Buddhism](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/buddhalaisuus) and [Confucianism](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/kungfutselaisuus), do not include belief in the supernatural.[^6]

#### Definition by reference to supernaturalism
Religion is sometimes defined as belief in [the supernatural](../articles/yliluonnollinen.md) and/or in supernatural beings. The definition by reference to supernaturalism is the definition of religion most commonly used in [religious studies](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/uskontotiede).[^7]

The problem with the definition is defining the supernatural.[^8] The content of this definition depends almost entirely on how the supernatural is defined.

#### Being nonreligious
The word nonreligious refers to something that is not based on religion or that has no religion in it. Since all people hold a faith-based view, all people are religious by the cognitive definition of religion. In the context of the cognitive definition, being nonreligious apparently means a position whose adherents are unaware of their own faith-based assumptions.
