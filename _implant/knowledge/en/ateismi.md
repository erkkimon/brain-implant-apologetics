# Atheism

*Topics: [Atheism](../topics/ateismi.md).*


**Atheism** means a worldview that lacks belief in God, or the [faith-based](../articles/usko-ja-tieto.md) view that [God](jumala.md) or gods do not exist. Depending on how religion is defined, atheism can be seen either as a non-religious or as a religious worldview. For example, under the [epistemic definition of religion](../articles/uskonnon-määritelmä.md) atheism is a religion, whereas under the [definition that refers to the supernatural](../articles/uskonnon-määritelmä.md) it is not. According to a study published in 2007, 5% of Finns classify themselves as atheists by conviction.[^1] Atheists are often [physicalists](../articles/fysikalismi.md).

Many atheists emphasise [free rational thinking](../articles/vapaa-ajattelijan-käsikirja.md), free from unjustified beliefs, and the importance of scientific knowledge for a worldview. Many atheists indeed consider themselves the most natural representatives of scientific thinking, and as a result they have named their view the [scientific worldview](../articles/tieteellinen-maailmankatsomus.md).

Most atheists have largely adopted the most fundamental Christian values and morality of our secular culture. Atheism in itself does not commit one to any particular view of morality. Most atheists are, however, secular [humanists](../glossary/sanasto.md#humanismi), in which case the measure of morality is taken to be, for example, humanity, or a being's capacity to experience suffering. On the basis of atheism, an absolute view of morality is in practice impossible to justify, which in extreme cases may lead, for example, to [nihilism](../glossary/sanasto.md#nihilismi). For this reason, the challenge for atheism becomes maintaining the values that arise from the Christian tradition in a secularising culture[^2].

In terms of scientific explanatory power, atheism generally appears quite convincing, although this is explained by the fact that natural science is restricted to studying only natural explanations. On questions concerning the existence of God, expertise in the natural sciences therefore does not provide the relevant knowledge; instead the question must be approached by the means of philosophy or theology. A lack of philosophical and theological training is, however, regrettably typical of many atheists who present themselves as experts on religion. Atheists who present themselves as experts are often natural scientists (e.g. [Richard Dawkins](richard-dawkins.md)) and thus not competent to assess, for example, the existence of God.

### Varieties of atheism

Atheism can be understood either as a [proposition](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/väitelause) or as an individual's [epistemic](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/tieto) attitude.[^3] Today atheists are often divided into [explicit atheism](../glossary/sanasto.md#eksplisiittinen-ateismi), which denies the existence of gods, and [implicit atheism](../glossary/sanasto.md#implisiittinen-ateismi), which is a lack of belief in the existence of gods but lacks an explicit denial of their existence.[^4] Implicit atheism differs from [agnosticism](../glossary/sanasto.md#agnostismi) in that the agnostic refuses to take a position on the existence of gods, whereas for the implicit atheist the concept "god" does not even exist.

Although most atheists hold views such as [humanism](../glossary/sanasto.md#humanismi)[^5] and [naturalism](../glossary/sanasto.md#naturalismi),[^6] there is no system of ideas common to all atheists.[^7] As a consequence of the definition of atheism, what unites all atheists is little more than criticism of other religions. Because atheism is merely the denial of other positions, positive grounds are seldom offered for atheism itself.

The only feature common to all atheists is the absence of belief in God, but the reasons and justifications for this view differ from one another. Since atheism is generally understood as the denial of the existence of gods, it essentially involves a conception of the nature and attributes of the deities being denied. The difficulty of an unambiguous [definition of atheism](ateismin-määritelmä.md) is thus tied to the phenomenon's many historical manifestations and the concept's many different interpretations.[^8]

## Justifications for atheism
For the explicit atheist, rational thinking and scientific knowledge usually hold a central place. For many atheists, however, atheism rests on an image of being scientific rather than on justifications that withstand criticism. In reality, atheism does not follow from scientific results.[^9] The image of being scientific is nevertheless strong, and the atheist often does not question, or even see, his deep [properly basic beliefs](../glossary/sanasto.md#perususkomus).
An atheist may feel that science supports atheism mainly in the sense that the atheist himself regards atheism as the default position as long as science has not proven the existence of God.[^10] This choice is, however, not scientific but [metaphysical](../glossary/sanasto.md#metafysiikka) or religious.

### Predictions of atheism

Atheism makes hardly any claims that can be tested directly by observation, because its only claims are, by definition, denials of theistic claims. Therefore it does not predict anything very well either, and so does not explain anything well. On the other hand, atheism could also be compatible with the predictions of many theistic claims, in which case atheism predicts things very weakly. The empirical predictions of atheism can, however, be formulated as a modus tollens inference:

* (1) a → b
* (2) ¬b
* (JP) ¬a

Where a is theism, and the prediction of atheism is ¬b.

#### Lack of justifications

*Main article: [Falsifiability of atheism](ateismin-falsifioitavuus.md)*

*Main article: [Existence of God](jumalan-olemassaolo.md)*

We could, for example, conclude that God does not exist if we assumed the premises:

* (1) "If God exists, then there are good justifications for the existence of God."
* (2) "There are no good justifications for the existence of God."

The theist or agnostic must question at least one of the premises, and from the perspective of Christian apologetics at least the 2nd premise is questioned. Even the atheist hardly denies that justifications for the existence of God have been presented, but in his opinion they are simply not good. The problem is surely what each person considers a sufficiently good justification. What matters for the argument, however, is not what 'Antti Atheist' in particular considers a good justification for the existence of God; rather, how convincing the required justification must be depends solely on how good the justifications are whose existence should follow from the existence of God. Moreover, the existence of justifications does not even imply that the atheist would be aware of all of them. For example, [Antony Flew](antony-flew.md), who was widely versed in the philosophy of religion, came towards the end of his life to find the justifications for the existence of God he encountered so convincing that he gave up his atheism.

The 1st premise, in turn, can be questioned quite easily, for example within a deistic conception of God.[^11] The first premise can also be questioned within Christianity, for example on the grounds that people [naturally believe in the existence of God](../articles/tuomas-kangasniemi-lapset-keksivät-uskon-jumaliin-aikuisten-opetuksesta-riippumatta.md). We do not need separate justifications for many of our other natural beliefs either, for example for the reliability of our perceptions or for the existence of persons outside our own mind. If belief in God is a human being's natural starting assumption, then perhaps it is precisely unbelief that requires justification, and so justified belief in God does not necessarily need further justifications in addition. A Christian can also question the first premise partially. He need not deny that knowledge of justifications defending God's existence follows from the existence of God. The Christian can instead deny that these justifications would have to be so good that they would convince the atheist.

Because both premises are so questionable, this argument does not as such work as a good justification, at least not for positive atheism, but only as a justification against a certain kind of conception of God, justifying at best only negative atheism or agnosticism. The inference is nevertheless formally valid, so if the premises were true, the conclusion would follow from them: "it is not the case that God exists." For the argument to justify the conclusion, however, its defender would have to present credible justifications for the plausibility of the premises.

For this reason, the justifications for atheism are often little more than criticism of the justifications for other faith-based positions. But criticism of other positions does not prove one's own position, unless one's own position is merely the denial of the criticised position. Atheism, however, is not the same as the claim "Christianity is not true", so the evidence for atheism could often just as well be interpreted as evidence for, say, deism. Atheism appears to be at bottom more of a faith-based position than, for example, Christianity, which makes many claims about various areas and whose detailed claims have been confirmed to a significant degree, for example [historically](jeesuksen-ylösnousemus.md) and [archaeologically](raamatun-historiallinen-luotettavuus.md).

From the perspective of Christian apologetics, there are no grounds for holding even the 2nd premise true, namely that "There are no good justifications for the existence of God". In practice the problem seems to come down to what people personally consider "good justifications", and this is just the kind of situation we could expect on the basis of many branches of Christianity: God's wish is not primarily to make us believe in his existence, but to bring us into a loving fellowship with him based on free will. The world offers sufficient grounds for belief in God if a person wants to be in fellowship with God, but at the same time a person can also believe in atheism if he so wishes.

#### Criticism of miracles

*Main article: [Miracle](ihme.md)*

The atheism of many atheists cannot be shown to be false, because they do not consider evidence against their atheism reliable. Against [miracles](ihme.md), atheists often present [David Hume's](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hume) <!-- red link: David Hume --> flawed argument that it is not reasonable to believe in miracles because they are extremely rare (for example: "extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence"). Even the most reliable historical evidence can be questioned in some way, and direct observations, experiences and physical evidence can always be dismissed as mistakes or fraud.

It is of course true, as David Hume said, that "No testimony is sufficient to establish a miracle, unless the testimony be of such a kind, that its falsehood would be more miraculous, than the fact, which it endeavours to establish." This holds for any claim: evidence cannot show a claim to be true unless the truth of the claim's opposite would be even more improbable, given the evidence. This does not, however, help to show that the evidence for a miracle, such as the [resurrection of Jesus](jeesuksen-ylösnousemus.md), is poor, unless the miracle is assumed from the outset to be arbitrarily improbable. But that is exactly what the argument was supposed to prove!

#### Belief in God is not scientific

*Main article: [Scientism](../articles/tieteisusko.md)*

In addition, atheism is often combined with scientism: science is today defined so that only natural causes are accepted as scientific explanations. Because of its naturalistic presupposition, then, the scientific community does not accept as reliable any explanations or evidence that would point to the supernatural. In other words, the scientistic atheist engages in a circular argument, called scientism, that sustains his atheism:

1. Evidence against atheism would require [supernatural](../articles/yliluonnollinen.md) explanations.
1. There is no reliable evidence for supernatural things, so supernatural explanations are not scientific.
1. The supernatural is not scientific, so the evidence is not scientific.
1. Because the evidence is not scientific, it is not reliable.
1. So there is no reliable evidence for supernatural things.

With this circular reasoning the scientistic atheist thus makes sure that he will never have to take any evidence against atheism seriously.

Scientistic atheism is therefore in reality not falsifiable, let alone verifiable. The epistemic value of scientism is therefore very close to zero. The atheism of many adherents of scientism is merely circular reasoning that follows from the current naturalistic philosophy of science, but which, through its scientific and reasonable-seeming argumentation, makes possible an extremely closed attitude.

#### Shifting the burden of proof onto theists
* Main article: [Burden of proof](../articles/todistuksen-taakka.md)
Atheists often argue that believers should show that God exists. If this cannot be done, the atheist often claims that it is not reasonable to believe in the existence of God. The atheist view has been defended, for example, as follows: it is absurd to demand that it be proven that God does not exist. Proving that something does not exist is not possible. Therefore those who claim that God exists have the duty to prove it, that is, they bear the burden of proof. If proof cannot be given, there is no reason to believe in the existence of God.[^12]

This claim is, however, highly problematic, because to the extent that atheism claims something, it should justify its claim; mere criticism of the theists' justifications is not a sufficient ground for atheism.[^13] The claim that one should not believe in something in the absence of evidence is false. For example, our beliefs in the reliability of our thinking, in our own existence, in mathematics (which starts from assumed axioms), and in the existence of other conscious beings lack justification, yet we are still justified in believing them. In fact, behind almost any claim lie unproven assumptions. The atheist thus applies his criterion, which leads to paralysing scepticism, selectively, directing his destructive, over-critical criterion, in an intellectually dishonest way, only at the target he chooses.

Moreover, atheists themselves in practice believe in beings whose existence they cannot prove: presumably every atheist would sign up to believing the proposition "there exists at least one human being whom God has not created." Now, however, the theist can point out that no justifications have been given for this claim, and if no such justifications are forthcoming, one must believe the negation of the proposition to be true: "There exists no human being whom God has not created." In other words, the burden of proof lies with both the atheist and the theist.

#### The number of religions
Atheists often take the number of religions as a justification for atheism. This view is, however, problematic in several ways. First, according to the [epistemic definition](../articles/uskonnon-määritelmä.md) of religion, atheism is just one religious view among others, so the number of religious views lowers the credibility of atheism just as much as that of the other positions. (Being equally blinkered, a theist could likewise argue that since there are many religions, at least atheism is not right.)

[George H. Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._Smith) has presented a classification of atheist views into existentialist, metaphysical, epistemological, practical, psychological, social, linguistic and [objectivist](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Objektivismi_(Rand)) atheism.[^14] It is interesting that while atheists often use the number of different branches of Christianity as 'evidence' against Christianity, they do not apply the same criterion to atheism, whose different branches should likewise count as a justification against atheism. In fact it seems clear that the branches of Christianity agree with each other on more things (the existence of God, the historicity and resurrection of Jesus, the divinity of Jesus, heaven, hell, the existence of right and wrong, accountability to God, ...)  than the branches of atheism do (they do not even agree on the definition of atheism: the claim that there is no God, or the lack of belief in God), so by this atheist criterion Christianity would be more probable than atheism.

#### The problem of evil
* Main article: [The problem of evil](../articles/pahan-ongelma.md)
One of the atheists' main criticisms of Christianity is the problem of evil. That is, how could a good, loving and almighty God permit evil, such as suffering?

1. If God is good, he would remove evil if only he could.
1. If he is also almighty, he is able to remove evil.
1. If a good and almighty God exists, there would be no evil in the world.
1. Yet there is evil in the world.

The existence of evil is hard to explain for every worldview. Harder still, for every worldview, is to comfort the one who suffers.

Christianity has several good internal answers to the intellectual problem of evil. First, God has given human beings freedom, including freedom to do evil, so that true love is possible. Evil helps a person come to the good God. And in the end evil is defeated, so that the outcome is both the good and an appreciation of God's goodness through the experience of evil. In addition, Christianity has good internal answers for the one suffering from evil: in Jesus, God himself came to suffer on earth, so he knows how a suffering person feels.
Although Christianity's answers do not remove the hard experience of evil or the often very great suffering, it gives them intellectually satisfying answers.

In fact, physicalist atheism's own answer to evil is completely untenable from the standpoint of experiencing suffering: evil does not even exist in objective reality. In fact, there is neither evil nor good; our experience is simply such that some things are perceived as evil and others as good. Our experience, in turn, is merely an illusion produced by a certain kind of neural process. Viewed objectively, even in a tsunami particles merely rearranged themselves. Your relative died? That means nothing more than that slightly less current now runs through the nervous system that constitutes him, and this produces a different reaction in your nervous system. Does life feel meaningless? That feeling is merely a reflection of a neurological phenomenon, which ultimately results from the aimless arrangement of particles and their regular yet purposeless motion. For this reason the problem of experiencing suffering may well be even harder for atheism than for Christianity.

### Atheism and metaethics

### Human problems of atheism

In practice, many things follow from atheism that run strongly against people's everyday experience and intuitions. The atheist can of course, for example, deny the value of intuition as a source of knowledge, in which case the following list need not cause any intellectual problem at all. The theist, on the other hand, can regard intuition, for example, as a good starting point which it is reasonable to believe until some independent evidence against it is found. In any case, these are things by which both the theist and the atheist live, even if the atheist does not actually believe in them:

**Human dignity: It is difficult, if not impossible, for the atheist to justify the value of a human being. For the physicalist atheist, only the material level is ultimate. The motion of particles knows nothing of human dignity, and a human being is ultimately nothing more than a complex collection of molecules. Typically the atheist does appeal to human dignity and assumes absolute human dignity, but this is borrowed from Christianity, since human dignity cannot be justified under atheism.**
**The reliability of reason: Typically the atheist thinks that thoughts are entirely the result of electrochemical reactions in the brain, so human thinking is not the outcome of a rational process but of statistically determined phenomena at the material level. It is hard for the atheist to justify why his thoughts in particular, and so atheism, should be true, insofar as his thoughts are entirely determined by the material level. If the atheist believes in the theory of evolution, this forms a second [defeater for atheism](../articles/alvin-plantinga-evoluutioteoreettinen-argumentti-naturalismia-vastaan.md).**
**[Free will](../articles/vapaa-tahto.md) and moral choices: According to the physicalist atheist, all our actions result from preceding physical conditions governed by the laws of nature, plus a dash of chance. The human mind, then, is not guided by any spiritual agent; all our actions happen by physical necessity. If you give in and do something against your principles, you could not even have chosen otherwise. You simply had bad luck. Considering another person bad because of immoral behaviour is then just as intolerant as considering him bad because of the colour of his eyes. He could not have acted otherwise, and so he cannot be held responsible for his deeds.**
**The irrationality of good and evil: For the physicalist atheist, the material level is ultimate. The motion of particles knows nothing of right and wrong, good and evil. According to Hume's guillotine, we also cannot infer how we ought to act from what kind of physical facts we observe. The good is merely a groundless opinion, produced by experience, about what is worth striving for in life. In practice the atheist wants to avoid anxiety and suffering and to attain happiness and pleasure. He has, however, no rational reason why he ought to do so. Thus for the atheist the good says less about the world than about the neural phenomena in his own head.**
**Lack of purpose: If the world or human beings were not created, our existence has no purpose. One can of course find content for life and set oneself various goals, but atheism offers a person no answer if life feels meaningless.**
**Lack of a lasting goal: According to present-day science, the universe faces heat death, and humanity and life on Earth will probably die out much earlier. In this light it is hard to find any lasting meaning or direction for life on the basis of atheism. The atheist usually has some kind of goal in life, but this is at most a subjective inclination towards temporary aims. Everything the atheist considers good, such as the diversity of nature or human happiness, will in any case disappear sooner or later.**
**Totalitarianism: It follows from the above that under atheism almost all the things that matter to people are arbitrarily chosen and without grounds. So nothing holds the atheist back from becoming an [anarchist](../glossary/sanasto.md#anarkismi), which on a larger scale can at times lead to destructive disintegration of society. Atheism's answer to this disintegration has typically been to force the masses into a particular way of thinking and acting,[^15] by any means necessary, since there is no responsible morality.**

#### Atheism and communism
Atheism as part of communism appears to lead to totalitarianism for three reasons:
1. [Intellectual arrogance](../articles/tapio-puolimatka-onko-tiede-kumonnut-jumalan.md), in which one's own anti-religious position is seen as the only option for an intelligent person.
1. As atheism spreads, the crumbling of the foundation of morality creates a need to force people, who no longer have an intellectually justified reason to act in unison, to act in the way those in power see as right.
1. Atheist rulers have no intellectual reasons to limit the pursuit of their own interests and their striving for power.

For the same reasons, atheism in communism appears also to lead to restrictions on discussion, so that other positions find it hard to get their justifications heard. In this way atheism typically shields itself from criticism, and the problems of communism are noticed only when atheist social structures collapse, accompanied by enormous human suffering, as happened in the Soviet Union and partly in China.
