# The historical reliability of the Bible

*Topics: [History](../topics/historia.md), [Bible](../topics/raamattu.md).*

*Image: thumbnail*

The [Bible](raamattu.md) is anchored to known times, places, rulers and persons, about which enough information exists for us to know that the text is reliable in its main features.

It is enormously valuable that the Bible, and thereby Christianity, contains many historical details through which they expose themselves to checking and refutation. This is quite different from many mainly philosophical religions not tied to history, which do not expose themselves to falsification, as in [atheism](ateismi.md), Buddhism, etc., but are merely theoretical claims among others. In the case of Christianity, every find in harmony with the text greatly strengthens the credibility of the whole Bible. In terms of hypothesis testing, this means that Christianity makes many detailed, strict claims, that is, predictions, and verifying even one of them raises the probability of Christianity significantly.

It should further be noted that historical research relies precisely on details when it assesses the reliability of sources. Most details of any long historical text cannot be verified, but on the strength of just some verified details the whole text can usually be considered reliable; for the author of the text could not influence which details we are able to verify, so the reliability of some details can usually be generalized to the whole text fairly soon.

### Historical and archaeological evidence

In countless places the Bible has exposed itself to checking. When one passage is confirmed, critics simply move on to questioning some other passage.

*Image: An image from the Leningrad Codex, a well-known manuscript of the Old Testament*

#### Old Testament

1. Abraham's home city Ur is one of the best-known ancient ruined cities of southern Babylonia, thanks to the excavations carried out by Sir Leonard Woolley in 1922-1934.[^1]
1. In the clay-tablet library found at Ebla in northern Syria (around 2300 BC), many names have been found that resemble the place names and names of rulers mentioned in ([Gen. 14](../books/gen.md)).[^1]
1. The so-called Mari documents from the 18th century BC mention Nahor, the home town of Rebekah.[^1]
1. The earliest mention of Israel outside the Bible is on the famous stele of Merneptah (c. 1224 BC).[^1]
1. The Bible was considered unreliable because it mentioned the people of the [Hittites](../articles/heettiläiset.md) on several occasions, although there was no other mention of this people. Plenty of evidence of the Hittites has since been found.
1. Bible critics and liberal critics have questioned the existence of King David. In 1993 a stone tablet written by Israel's enemies (the Tel Dan Stele) was found, which mentions the defeat of a king of the royal house of David (Jehoram, son of Ahab) [^2] The Mesha inscription find also confirms David's existence by mentioning the house of David, and in addition it mentions other biblical names such as Omri ( ([1 Kings 16:28](../books/1ki.md))).
1. Radiocarbon dating supports the idea that the huge mines in Jordan are the same ones that, according to the Bible, were controlled by King David and his son Solomon[^3].
1. In 1938 Nelson Glueck found the copper smelters of Israel's King Solomon at Ezion-geber. Phoenician inscriptions have provided further information about Solomon's fleet of Tarshish ships (1 Kings 10:22).[^1]
1. The seal of King Hezekiah of Judah has been found in Jerusalem.[^4]
1. In 1947, together with the other so-called Dead Sea Scrolls, a scroll containing the whole book of Isaiah was found at Qumran; it was a thousand years older than the oldest manuscripts known until then.[^1]

*Image: Text from the Codex Alexandrinus, a well-known manuscript of the New Testament*

#### [New Testament](../articles/uusi-testamentti.md)

> For the study of the original text of the New Testament, 274 majuscule manuscripts, 2,795 minuscule manuscripts, about 2,200 lectionaries and 88 papyri and ostraca are available.
>
> — [^1]

1. The ossuary of the high priest Caiaphas has been found.[^5] Caiaphas is possibly mentioned in another burial find as well.[^6]
1. Even Pilate's existence came to be doubted, but then his name, his position and the fact that he was governor in the time of Emperor Tiberius were found on a stone slab[^7]. Information about Pilate has also been obtained from old coins.[^1]
1. In skeletal remains we see details that correspond to the crucifixion account.
1. The Gospel of Luke mentions ( ([Luke 3:1](../books/luk.md))) a tetrarch named Lysanias in Abilene around AD 27. Liberal critics and skeptics long regarded this as a clear error in the Bible, because Lysanias was not a tetrarch but the ruler of Chalcis half a century earlier. Since then a dedicatory inscription has been found, dating from the time of Tiberius, AD 14-37, which mentions Lysanias the tetrarch of Abilene [^8]. So the Bible is speaking of a different Lysanias.
1. Skeptics have also questioned the existence of Nazareth, because no evidence of it had been found. Archaeologists have now found a list recording families of Jewish priests who were resettled as a result of the destruction of the Jerusalem temple, which Jesus foretold ( ([Luke 21:5-6](../books/luk.md))). For one family, the place of settlement recorded is the town of Nazareth. Although Nazareth was an insignificant town, there is not much doubt among archaeologists even about its location.
1. Papyri show that in the Roman Empire people had to go to be registered during a census in the place their family came from.[^1] This fits Luke's account of the birth of Jesus.
1. According to Luke, the census took place while Quirinius was governor of Syria, during the reign of Herod the Great. This created a problem, because Herod died in 4 BC and Quirinius is known to have begun as governor only in AD 6. Since then, however, a coin has been found bearing Quirinius's name and the information that he served as proconsul of Syria and Cilicia from 11 BC until after Herod's death. So there were apparently two men named Quirinius.
1. ([Acts 17:6](../books/act.md)) mentions "politarchs", the officials of the city of Thessalonica. Skeptics claimed that Luke had made a mistake, because the term politarchs was not found in other documents. Later a dedicatory inscription was found on an archway that began: "In the time of the politarchs...". Today more than thirty inscriptions mentioning politarchs have been found, several of them from Thessalonica from the same period that Luke writes about [^9].
1. The Acts of the Apostles mentions [83 further facts confirmed by historical and archaeological research](apostolien-teot.md).
1. The text about the pool of Bethesda ( ([John 5:1-15](../books/jhn.md))) was widely questioned until the pool was found exactly as the Gospel of John describes it (five colonnades). Likewise the pool of Siloam ( ([John 9:7](../books/jhn.md))) has been found, as have Jacob's well ( ([John 4:12](../books/jhn.md))), the stone pavement by the Jaffa Gate where Jesus was brought before Pilate ( ([John 19:13](../books/jhn.md))), and Pilate's identity [^10].

The theologian and archaeologist Eero Junkkaala has described the relationship between archaeology and the Bible as follows:

> So what does the Bible look like in the light of this research?
> The overall picture is completely clear: we are dealing with a historical book.
> The names of fifteen kings of Israel and Judah in all are also found in texts outside the Bible. A mudbrick gate from the time of Abraham has been excavated at Dan, and the gates built by Solomon at Hazor, Megiddo and Gezer, which fits excellently with the mention of Solomon in 1 Kings 9:15. The altar built by King Jeroboam has been found, and the ruins of a palace from the time of King Ahab have been excavated in Samaria. An exceptionally large number of ivory objects were found in them, which fits remarkably well with the description in Amos 6:4… The Bible is thus part of the real history of the Near East.

### The internal evidential force of the text

Moreover, the biblical text tells of events directly, without attaching legendary exaggeration or theological meanings to them. The falls and faults of even important persons in the events are told without embellishment (compare, for example, King David's adultery and murder ( ([2 Sam. 11](../books/2sa.md)))). Events that are hard to explain are also told without added explanations.

Below are ten reasons that point to the reliability of the New Testament writings[^11]:

1. The New Testament writers told humiliating things about themselves.
   * They are dull-witted and often do not understand what Jesus says. ( ([Luke 18:34](../books/luk.md)),  ([John 12:16](../books/jhn.md)))
   * They are hard-hearted ( ([Mark 14:32-41](../books/mrk.md)))
   * They are rebuked. Jesus, for example, calls Peter Satan ( ([Matt. 16:23](../books/mat.md))).
   * They were cowards. All the disciples except one hide when Jesus goes to the cross.
   * They were doubters. Jesus said many times that he would rise from the dead, but the disciples doubt when they hear of the resurrection ( ([Matt. 28:17](../books/mat.md))).
1. The New Testament writers recorded problematic sayings of Jesus and embarrassing details
   * His mother and brothers thought Jesus was "out of his mind". ( ([Mark 3:21](../books/mrk.md)))
   * He was considered a "deceiver". ( ([John 7:14](../books/jhn.md)))
   * He was crucified
1. They kept Jesus's difficult sayings in
   * ([Matt. 5:28](../books/mat.md))
   * ([Matt. 5:29-42](../books/mat.md))
   * ([Matt. 6:19-21](../books/mat.md))
   * ([Matt. 7:1-2](../books/mat.md))
1. The New Testament writers clearly distinguished Jesus's words from their own.
1. The writers kept details about the resurrection that they would not have invented.
   * Jesus was buried in the tomb of Joseph of Arimathea, a member of the Sanhedrin that had condemned him.
   * Women were the first witnesses of the resurrection.
   * The conversions of priests - these were easy for the opposing side to check.
   * The Jews' explanations of what had happened.
1. The texts tell of historical persons, more than 30 of whom have been historically confirmed from other sources.
   * It would have been difficult to falsify things by telling the truth only about those persons for whom we find confirmation.
1. The texts contain divergent details.
1. The writers challenge their readers to check the facts, even about miracles.
1. The writers tell of miracles as they do of other historical events, simply, without emphasis or exaggeration.
1. The writers abandoned their beliefs and customs, took on new ones, and did not deny their testimony under persecution and in death.

In this way, through historical reliability too, the worldview the Bible conveys is anchored to reality in a quite special way.
