# Icons of Evolution (book)

*Topics: [Books](../topics/kirjat.md), [Theory of evolution](../topics/evoluutioteoria.md).*

**Icons of Evolution** is a book by the American [biologist](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/biologia) [Jonathan Wells](../pages/jonathan-wells.md), in which he examines the most important pieces of evidence used in marketing the [theory of evolution](evoluutioteoria.md). Wells began his biology studies as an [evolutionist](evoluutioteoria.md) and began to doubt the theory only while writing his doctoral dissertation on cell and developmental biology. The first thing he noticed was the widely used embryo picture comparisons, which have long been known to be forgeries. Of the 10 biology textbooks Wells examined, eight swallowed this "evidence" whole, and even the remaining two presented it as strongly misleading<!-- Halutaanko pikemminkin sanoa: "voimakkaan harhaanjohtavasti"? Wellsin voi kritiikissään sanoa esittävän tämän "todisteen" "voimakkaan harhaanjohtavana", mutta lienevätkö nämä kaksi oppikirjaa hänen kanssaan tässä kohden samalla vai eri linjalla?-->. This embryo comparison picture also stubbornly persists in Finnish upper secondary school biology textbooks.[^1]

This article briefly presents the matters Wells deals with in his book; those interested in, for example, the technical details should get hold of *Icons of Evolution* itself. Finally, the article discusses the credibility problems connected with the marketing of the [theory of evolution](evoluutioteoria.md). Jonathan Wells is not a [creationist](../articles/kreationismi.md) in the narrow sense in which the term is usually understood. He does not believe in a so-called young earth, nor does he require a literal interpretation of [Genesis](../books/gen.md). Some [Darwinist](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darvinismi)-[apologists](../glossary/sanasto.md#apologeetta) have evidently found it hard to accept the undeniable fact that a real scientist can doubt the Darwinian theory of evolution for other than religious reasons. There are, however, several examples of scientists who have studied the theory of evolution and changed sides.

### Miller's experiment

Chapters dealing with the origin of life regularly refer to the experiment carried out by **[Stanley Miller](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Miller)** in 1953, in which Miller imitated the conditions of the supposed early earth and produced some amino acids. The problem with this evidence is its underlying assumption of a reducing early atmosphere. Much evidence suggests that the early atmosphere was oxidizing, which in turn would have been devastating for [origin-of-life scenarios](../articles/abiogeneesi.md). This is not the only problem with the evidential force of the experiment. Among other things, the escape of hydrogen into space was not taken into account in the experiment at all, which researchers regard as highly problematic.[^2]

### Darwin's "tree of life"

According to Wells, another myth repeated in the books is the model launched by **[Darwin](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin)**, according to which all forms of life have a common origin and development has proceeded so as to leave a tree-like "trace" in the history of life. The problems for this theory are the [Cambrian explosion](kambrikauden-räjähdys.md) and the "tree" built from molecular comparisons, which contradicts the "tree of life" based on comparative anatomy. Seen against the whole picture, the Cambrian happened at lightning speed and is better explained by a "fence picture" than by a "tree picture". Different major groups arose as if from nothing and continued in history as their own groups. A molecular-level match with the evolutionary tree of the traditional view was eagerly awaited. Research has, however, shown this hope to be a dead end.[^3]

### Homologies

Similar structures between two different species are often explained as a consequence of a common ancestor. At first glance this sounds reasonable, but taken as a whole, using homology as evidence for the theory of evolution is also difficult. First, if homology is defined as a consequence of a common origin, it cannot be used as evidence to demonstrate this common origin. Otherwise the reasoning is a so-called hermeneutic circle. Apparent similarity can, in fact, also result from so-called convergent evolution, where two organisms have developed independently of each other to look alike. Second, biologists have long known that homologous traits do not originate from the same genes, so the mechanism that produces them is unknown.[^4]

### Haeckel's embryo comparison

*Main article: [Recapitulation theory](../articles/rekapitulaatioteoria.md)*

The series of embryo comparisons that Ernst Haeckel forged in the 1800s has retained its position as evidence for evolution at the popular level, even though the "series" itself was determined to be a forgery as early as the 1920s. In Finland, even Tieteen kuvalehti published photographs of these embryos some time ago. Despite this, the series is still one of the grounds of many people's belief in evolution. In the same category belongs the commonly peddled misconception that the human embryo has "gills" or "gill rudiments".[^5]

### The lizard-bird Archaeopteryx

This is the most famous so-called transitional fossil, which is claimed to connect reptiles to birds and thus demonstrate macroevolution in practice. According to Wells, the use of this evidence is, however, misleading. This chapter in Wells' book is perhaps the most astonishing. Even a reader widely familiar with the evolution discussion might be surprised by Wells' claim that archaeopteryx is no longer considered a "missing link" in the latest research. In writing and criticizing each chapter, Wells was assisted by experts in the relevant field, far from all of whom shared Wells's attitude towards the theory of evolution. The task of these "observers" was mainly to comment on the factual basis of Wells's arguments. The reference group for the lizard-bird chapter included, among others, [Alan Feducia](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Feduccia), who needs no introduction even to those only superficially acquainted with the subject.[^6]

### Peppered moths

A classic example of evolution in practice is the reference to the change in color of peppered moth populations as a result of natural selection. This "evidence" has not played a significant part in the debate about the theory of evolution, because at best it is merely an example of so-called microevolution in practice. In any case, the peppered moth example is problematic even in this respect. Even the widely circulated images were taken of dead peppered moths, because living ones were never successfully photographed! Wells refers to the research of Finnish researcher K. Mikkola, according to whom peppered moths do not even rest on tree trunks, as the earlier view simply took for granted.[^7]

### Darwin's finches

The finch populations of the Galapagos Islands have traditionally been used as a good example of Darwinian evolution in practice. The structures of the finches' beaks differ from one another, and this is explained using the concepts of the standard theory of evolution. However, the research of Peter and Rosemary Grant has shown that the variation in the size of the finches' beaks oscillates back and forth with the weather, and the excellent survival of hybrid forms may in fact be better explained by the merging of several species than by speciation.

### Four-winged fruit flies

In this chapter, Wells deals with a typical Darwinian assumption, according to which mutations provide evolution with sufficient raw material for morphological development. As an example, Wells discusses the breeding of the four-winged fruit fly, which evolutionists regard as evidence. American geneticist Ed Lewis received the Nobel Prize in 1995 for "developing" the four-winged fruit fly. In some contexts, this "monster" has been used as evidence for evolution and of the power of mutations to produce morphological changes. Technically, the mutations in this case did indeed produce morphological changes; instead of two wings, there were four. According to Wells, using this as evidence for evolution is, however, misleading. In addition to the extra wings being non-functional, this creature was produced artificially by combining three kinds of mutant forms. By manipulating the gene affecting the development of the wings, the complex wing development process was disrupted, and the result was a monster fly barely able to fly. In the same chapter, Wells deals with typical arguments belonging to the same category in support of evolution. The occasional ability of bacterial populations to develop forms resistant to antibiotics is widely regarded as an example of evolution in practice. Most of this "evolution" does not actually occur through mutations, but rather the resistant forms are already part of the populations, or they acquire their resistance by "borrowing" from some other organism. In some cases a mutation really is involved. Wells, however, points out that this is by no means the "morphological mutation" required by the Darwinian model, and for this reason, evolutionists have sought support precisely from the previously mentioned four-winged fruit fly.

### The evolution of the horse

The argument concerning the evolution of the horse, according to Wells, has been interesting in that it was once used to support the notion of so-called guided evolution. This "guidance" was interpreted either as an innate vital force or, traditionally, as the handiwork of the Creator. Among others, arch-evolutionist G. G. Simpson vigorously opposed this view and wrote critically about the horse's lineage. Originally, this straight-line family tree came from the pen of paleontologist Othniel Marsh, who presented this famous piece of evidence in 1879. The old "basic line" Eohippus - Miohippus - Merychippus - Pliohippus - Equus had to be re-evaluated as early as the 1920s, when paleontologist William Matthew demonstrated that several extinct horse species had lived simultaneously with Eohippus. Also, the incorrect ordering of some forms in the strata confuses the lineage. Indeed, Miohippus appears in the fossil record before Mesohippus, whereas the theory requires the reverse order. For example, the supposed change in size is difficult to demonstrate from the fossil material when all horse fossils are taken into account. In this broader sample, no increase in size has been observed; instead, in some cases, the size has actually appeared to decrease<!-- Tieto kannattaa tarkistaa -->.[^8]

### From ape to man

Human evolution is the last of the icons treated by Wells. Science writer Henry Gee wrote in the journal Nature in 1999: "The time intervals between different fossils are so large that we cannot say anything definitive about their possible connection to one another through ancestry and descent." This describes Wells's approach to the world of human evolution. There are almost as many different viewpoints, classifications, and family trees as there are researchers. Wells admits that genuine fossils have been found and that some of these look ape-like and some human-like. The problem lies in defining the lines of development. The only connecting factor seems to be the conviction that this developmental course certainly took place. The gaping gaps between vague pieces of evidence are filled with fictional rhetoric, which paleoanthropologist Misia Landau compared to old folk tales. Wells quotes quite recent sources and documents the frustrated remarks of several specialists in the field regarding the mess of paleoanthropology.

### So what?

Marketing the theory of evolution with the sales arguments treated by Wells raises many questions. It is naturally easy to check whether these items Wells points to really are still in biology textbooks.

We can also ask whether Wells is right in considering these pieces of evidence "wrong, old, and/or misleading". This is, of course, one of the most essential questions in the whole debate. Some of them are indisputably wrong, and some of them hardly all enthusiastic evolutionists will agree to admit are wrong. This presentation by Wells provides a good basis for continuing the discussion. Each chapter can be treated separately, and Wells's bibliography offers a good entry point for research.

Since a closer look at these arguments and their persuasiveness will probably show Wells's skepticism to be justified, the question arises: is there better evidence for the theory of evolution? Then again, if better evidence exists, why is it not presented? Even if we assume that there is indisputable evidence in favor of the theory of evolution that is not generally known, from a marketing perspective this raises a new question. If a person who believes in evolution bases his belief on, say, these ten pieces of evidence, which (we are assuming) are false, can he regard his belief as anything other than "blind commitment" to a certain worldview?

On the other hand, it is possible that the authors of the books simply happen not to know about new directions in research — or is it? Kenneth Miller, Douglas Futuyma, and others are recognized experts in evolutionary research. If they do not know where things stand, then who does? Can the argument "scientists believe in evolution" be regarded as any evidence at all if these scientists do not in fact even know the evidence? Wells provides an interesting example related to this. The author of a textbook and a recognized evolutionary researcher, Douglas Futuyma, responded to a charge presented against him on a Kansas City internet forum, according to which Futuyma lied when using Haeckel's embryo drawings in his 1998 book *Evolutionary Biology*. Futuyma defended himself by stating that at the time of writing he was unaware of the errors in Haeckel's drawings. It is hardly unreasonable to ask: if a professional evolutionist who writes a university-level textbook, who has even written an anti-creationist book, does not know such a fact, can we consider his belief in evolution as an inevitable conclusion derived from scientific facts? Does the claim "almost all scientists believe in evolution" then have much weight at all? Since only a fairly small fraction of researchers actually study the theory of evolution, the opinions of the majority have little significance. A biochemist studying lactose bacteria does not need knowledge of macroevolution in their work.

Contrary to a claim sometimes presented, science lives and does well even without the theory of evolution. This claim can be justified, among other things, by the fact that evolutionists have encouraged scientists not to argue publicly with creationists. The reason for this is that these scientists are not sufficiently well-versed in the theory of evolution and its evidence. Anyone can read articles on the talkorigins site dealing with the subject by Eugenia Scott and Richard Trott. Futuyma is, however, a genuine evolutionary researcher, who can be assumed to be very well-versed in the study of the theory, its strengths and weaknesses. If such an expert bases his belief in the theory on vague claims, can the theory really be regarded as a "scientific fact comparable to gravity"?

### Why are there no anti-Darwinian articles in scientific publications?

A typical response to this question is either a reference by creationists to a conspiracy or a reference by evolutionists to the poor quality of the research and to the "fact" that evidence against evolution simply does not exist. It is a generally known fact that many anti-Darwinist researchers constantly publish articles in scientific journals, but when they write against the Darwinian theory, their articles are routinely rejected. It is difficult to believe that this stems only from the fact that suddenly the level of the authors' argumentation and basic research drops so significantly that publication becomes impossible. The matter can, however, be approached from another angle. Quoting the famous philosopher of science Karl Popper, the theory of evolution as a whole is a "metaphysical research programme". This theory, however, breaks down into different sub-theories and pieces of evidence or "icons", as Wells names them. Plenty of criticism of these various sub-theories can be found in scientific publications. Experts from various fields criticize the so-called evidence of their own research fields, even though at the same time they believe in the correctness of the theory of evolution. Below is a simple example from outside Wells' book.

Professor David Kitts, reviewing Pierre-Paul Grasse's book in the journal Paleobiology, states: "The fossil record does not even provide a single piece of evidence in support of Darwin's theory, except in the sense that the fossil record is compatible with it, just as it is compatible with other theories of evolution, revolutionary theories and special creationist theories, and even non-historical theories.[^9]" According to Kitts' text, the fossil record thus supports the theory of evolution no more than, for example, the theory of special creation. On the other hand, the French zoologist legend Pierre-Paul Grasse stated that fossils are the central evidence when studying the theory of evolution: "A naturalist must remember that the evolutionary process can be demonstrated only by means of fossil forms. Knowledge of paleontology is thus a prerequisite; only paleontology can provide him with evidence of evolution and reveal its direction or mechanism.[^10]"

Researcher A states that only X can provide evidence of evolution.
Researcher B states that X does not prove evolution.

Of course, a reasonable question arises as to why, for example, Kitts believes in evolution if the fossil record does not, in his view, support that theory. Apparently, Kitts considers evidence from some other field to be convincing and bases his belief on that. Grasse, on the other hand, sees paleontology as the only proper evidence for evolution. Can Grasse and Kitts both be evolutionists at the same time, in such a way that both their convictions that evolution is well founded are justified? We can naturally claim that Grasse is wrong, or that Kitts is wrong, or even that both are wrong. They may indeed be. The idea is, however, that both are renowned researchers whose criticism in no case arises from religious perspectives, a need to oppose the theory of evolution, or general ignorance. Why are special creationist articles needed, which claim that the fossil record can be explained just as well with a creation model, if authorities of Kitts's calibre make these statements anyway?

### In conclusion

This article has not shown the theory of evolution to be wrong. In fact, I have not even aimed at that. The subject of treatment has been the soundness of the arguments used as the basis for the evolutionist conviction. Perhaps next April a complete series of 8300 human fossils will be found, all of which have a distinctive trait of the same lineage and are found in the same area, in the right order. Perhaps someone will develop a hippopotamus from amino acids in a laboratory and so show macroevolution to be possible, at least as far as genetics is concerned. The future is open and science continuously makes new discoveries—and discards old theories. Thus, the main idea of the article is that the conviction of evolutionists is based less on facts than on a worldview commitment to the evolution myth instead of the creation myth.
