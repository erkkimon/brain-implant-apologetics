# The resurrection of Jesus

*Topics: [History](../topics/historia.md), [Jesus](../topics/jeesus.md), [Miracles](../topics/ihmeet.md).*

Christianity differs from other religions in that it rests essentially on a historical event, the resurrection of [Jesus](jeesus-nasaretilainen.md).[^1] There is a considerable number of historical mentions and descriptions of the resurrection, but the resurrection accounts of the Gospels of [Matthew](../books/mat.md),[^2] [Mark](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markuksen_evankeliumi)[^3] and [Luke](../articles/evankeliumi-luukkaan-mukaan.md)[^4] can be regarded as historically reliable, comprehensive accounts. What is essential in the resurrection accounts is that the tomb is found to be empty and the resurrection is offered as the explanation.

### The historicity of Jesus' resurrection

The resurrection of Jesus was a historical fact on which the early Christians built their lives:

> But if Christ has not been raised, your faith is without foundation and you are still in the power of your sins.
>
> — ([1 Cor. 15:17](../books/1co.md))

Biblical-critical and historical research on the resurrection of Jesus has been one of the most popular research topics of the last couple of centuries. It is therefore interesting that, despite repeated attempts, critical research has not managed to develop a single credible naturalistic theory for the historical facts concerning the resurrection of Jesus. New Testament scholars' views on the historical reliability of the New Testament vary, but scholars generally agree that there are some historical facts about the final phase of Jesus' life and the first phase of the Christian church that we can know with sufficient certainty.

**Gary Habermas**[^5] made a thorough study of what scholars of our time generally think about the resurrection. He examined over 1,400 of the most critical studies of the resurrection from the years 1975–2003. On the basis of his study, Habermas[^6] lists 12 historical facts related to the resurrection of Jesus on which the majority of scholars who specialise in this question are remarkably unanimous, regardless of their starting assumptions:

1. Jesus died by Roman crucifixion.
1. He was probably buried in a private tomb.
1. After Jesus' death the disciples were dejected, grieving and had lost hope.
1. Jesus' tomb was found to be empty very soon after the burial.
1. The disciples had experiences that they believed were appearances of the risen Jesus.
1. As a result of these experiences the disciples' lives were completely transformed, so that they were ready to die for their faith.
1. The bodily resurrection of Jesus was proclaimed very early in the history of the Christian church.
1. The disciples began to testify and preach publicly about Jesus' resurrection in the city of Jerusalem, where Jesus had been crucified and buried a short time before.
1. The message of the gospel centred on Jesus' death and resurrection.
1. Sunday became the Christians' main day of assembly and prayer, even though a large part of the early Christians were Jews who had been taught from childhood to keep the Sabbath holy as a day of rest.
1. Jesus' brother James, who had earlier been a sceptic, was converted when he believed he had met the risen Christ.
1. Only a few years later Saul of Tarsus (Paul) became a believing Christian as a result of an experience that he believed was an appearance of the risen Jesus.

Jesus' tomb was empty. If Jesus' opponents had stolen the body, they could have produced it and so refuted the Christians' claims. If Jesus' disciples had stolen the body, they would hardly have been ready to die in defence of their own lie. The New Testament reports twelve separate occasions on which Jesus appeared to his followers after his resurrection. These appearances were not hallucinations of a few seconds experienced by a single person, but situations in which several people encountered Jesus together. They touched him physically, ate and drank with him and talked about the kingdom of God.

> To them he also showed by many indisputable proofs that he was alive after his death. He appeared to them several times over forty days and spoke about the kingdom of God.
>
> — ([Acts 1:3](../books/act.md))

In the light of current research it is evident that no naturalistic theory can do justice to these historical facts. Neither the hallucination theory, the conspiracy theory, nor the theory that the resurrection is a legend that developed later out of Hellenistic culture and myths of the gods has withstood criticism. As **Wolfgang Pannenberg** puts it:

> These explanations have so far failed.
>
> — Wolfgang Pannenberg[^7]

Naturalistic biblical critics have taken turns refuting each other's theories. **David Strauss** convincingly refutes the theory put forward by **Karl Venturini** and **Heinrich Paulus** that Jesus did not really die but only lost consciousness. **Friedrich Schleiermacher** and Paulus point out many problems in the hallucination theory defended by Strauss and **Ernest Renan**. **Theodor Keim** is regarded as having refuted the hallucination theory for good. The theories of the history-of-religions school, that the views about Jesus' resurrection developed out of Hellenistic myths of the gods, have crumbled in the light of the facts. Nor has any combination of naturalistic theories proved workable. At present the only intellectually satisfying explanation is that Jesus rose bodily from the dead.

Naturalistic scholars have found it hard to find a tenable naturalistic explanation for the historical facts concerning the resurrection of Jesus. In the case of the resurrection, the undeniable historical facts are many, and together they form a mutually supporting whole. Biblical critics who do their research from very different starting assumptions agree that the naturalistic theories about the historical facts of Jesus' resurrection have failed. **Raymond E. Brown** gives a detailed list of naturalistic theories about the resurrection and says that no scholar with scientific self-respect can defend them any longer.

> These clumsy theories are no longer worth taking seriously – – Occasionally some new variant of the conspiracy theory captures the public imagination for a short while, but serious scholars pay little attention to these imaginative reconstructions.
>
> — Raymond E. Brown [^8]

The historical evidence for the resurrection is convincing because eyewitnesses have brought forward undeniable historical facts which together make it impossible to devise a naturalistic explanation for them. All early Christian thinking was marked by reliance on eyewitness testimony. The Church Father **Papias** stresses the importance of living eyewitnesses for obtaining reliable historical knowledge.

> Whenever someone came who had followed those elders, I inquired into their words: what Andrew or Peter or Philip or Thomas or James or John or Matthew or any other of the Lord's disciples had said, and what Aristion and the presbyter John say. For I supposed that I did not profit so much from books as from the living and abiding word.
>
> — Eusebius, Church History[^9]

The most important eyewitnesses were of course the 12 disciples, who were with Jesus from the beginning of his public ministry until his ascension into heaven. Both the Gospel of Luke and the Gospel of John stress that the disciples could be reliable witnesses of Jesus' life and death because they had followed his life closely throughout his public ministry.

> You too are my witnesses, for you have been with me from the beginning.
>
> — ([John 15:27](../books/jhn.md))

> Many have already undertaken to draw up accounts of the things that have taken place among us, as they were told to us by those who from the beginning were eyewitnesses and became servants of the word.
>
> — ([Luke 1:1-2](../books/luk.md))

The disciples' task was to see to it that what was known about Jesus was passed on to new Christians in its original and reliable form. They were the guarantee of the truth of what the gospel told, since they were ready to die rather than deny the gospel. Putting the Gospels into writing aimed to preserve the eyewitnesses' testimony in reliable form also for the people who would live after the eyewitnesses had died. The names of the disciples are carefully listed in each of the Synoptic Gospels, because the Gospels were built on their testimony. When a Gospel names less important people, it is usually because the Gospels rest on their testimony at that point.[^10]

### The reliability of the resurrection accounts

The resurrection accounts have quite often been claimed to be legends.[^11] The formation of a legend, however, takes several generations. In his book *Roman Law and Roman Society in the New Testament* the historian **N. A. Sherwin-White** points out that the source works on Roman history are usually biased and written at least a generation after the events. Yet from these works one can still work out in broad outline what really happened.[^12] By a practically complete consensus of biblical critics, the Gospels were written and put into circulation before even one generation had died out, while the eyewitnesses were still alive.[^13] So there are no chronological grounds for claiming that the text is legendary.

When the resurrection accounts are examined text-critically, it can be seen that the texts themselves give no reason to read them as legends. The simplicity and sparseness of the accounts speak for the accuracy of what they describe. The account is not embellished with theological or apologetic argument that would give reason to suspect the story was shaped to suit the author's motives.[^14]

If the story had been invented or embellished, women would hardly have been chosen as the witnesses of Jesus' resurrection. In the Judaism of the beginning of our era, women were generally considered frivolous and given to gossip.[^15] Women's standing is shown by sayings current among rabbinic Jews, according to which "*the words of the Torah should rather be burned than fall into a woman's hands*" or "*let a Jewish man thank God daily that he was not created a woman*".[^16] Women's testimony was considered worthless[^17], nor were they accepted as witnesses in court.[^18] Yet all the Synoptic Gospels name women as the first eyewitnesses at the empty tomb[^19], and not even this detail has been glossed over in the Synoptists' resurrection accounts.

One rather convincing piece of evidence for the historicity of the resurrection is the existence of Christianity. There would be no Christianity if the apostles had not once proclaimed the good news of the risen Jesus. They would not have proclaimed a risen Jesus if they had not believed their own proclamation. Why not? Because almost all the apostles died as martyrs for their proclamation. It is quite absurd to suppose that they held on to an invented story all the way to their execution. So for all practical purposes the apostles certainly believed in the resurrection of Jesus. A belief a person is ready to die for is not a product of the imagination. The hypothesis that they went mad is made absurd by the number of people who would have had to go mad.

### Was the tomb empty?
If anything can be considered certain in historical research, it is that the tomb really was empty, and most scholars in the field hold this view.[^20] One significant piece of evidence for the empty tomb is the existence of Christianity. Christianity is the result of the apostles proclaiming that the tomb had been emptied, and this proclamation was the result of the apostles believing that the tomb was empty. Even sceptics must admit that there would be no Christianity if the apostles had not believed the tomb was empty. It is senseless to think the apostles were lying, because they were crucified, fed to lions and beheaded for their belief. Dying for a lie you made up yourself requires nothing less than "flipping out", and it is extremely unlikely that all the apostles who were killed had gone mad.

It is highly significant that even the early Jewish arguments concede that the tomb was empty. The Jewish arguments start from the assumption that the tomb was empty, and they try to explain its emptying naturally. A good example of such argument is in chapter 28 of [the Gospel of Matthew](../books/mat.md).

> While the women were on their way, some of the guards went into the city and told the chief priests everything that had happened. Then these met together with the elders and decided to give the soldiers a large sum of money. They said to the soldiers: "Say that his disciples came during the night while you were asleep and stole him. And if this reaches the governor's ears, we will certainly pacify him and see to it that you suffer no trouble." The soldiers took the money and did as they had been told. Their story has been spread among the Jews to this day.
>
> — Matt. 28:11-15

According to the Gospel of Matthew, the Jews did not even try to claim that Jesus was in the tomb, but gave an alternative explanation for the tomb's emptying. The authenticity of Matthew's narrative is also supported by, among other things, the Jewish anti-gospel *[Toledot Jesu](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledot_Yeshu)*, according to which the tomb was empty because the gardener had taken Jesus out of his tomb and buried him in his garden.[^21] In addition, in the 2nd-century[^22] debate between **Trypho** and **[Justin Martyr](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinos_Marttyyri) <!-- red link: Justinus Marttyyri -->**, Trypho claims that the tomb is empty because the disciples had stolen the body.[^23] Tertullian confirms that this was the Jewish explanation for the empty tomb in his best-known work, *[Apologeticus](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apologeticus)*, written in 197.[^24]

### Alternative theories for the empty tomb

Although most scholars accept that the empty tomb is almost certain within the framework of historical research, many scholars, because of their worldview, do not want to admit that anything supernatural was involved in the emptying of the tomb. The alternative theories are in fact evidence that even sceptics are convinced the tomb was empty, and they result from the need to explain this practical fact in some way other than supernaturally.

#### The apostles made up the story themselves

Suppose the apostles had developed a story out of their own imagination. How long would anyone keep claiming their story was true if they were tortured for years? And how long would anyone go on after some of their friends who spread the same story had already been killed for refusing to admit they were lying?
