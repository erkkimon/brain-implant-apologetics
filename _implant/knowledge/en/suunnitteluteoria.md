# Design theory

*Topics: [Design theory](../topics/suunnitteluteoria.md).*


> One can always claim that the death of a man lying with five bullet holes through his chest was natural after all. Then again, it is hardly a far-fetched conclusion either that his fate was a deliberate, planned killing that had a perpetrator.
>
> — Gregory Koukl

According to **design theory**, or **ID theory** (intelligent design theory, *älyllisen suunnittelun teoria*), some features or structures [in nature](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/luonto) are better explained by an intelligent cause than by an unguided natural process such as [natural selection](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/luonnonvalinta).[^1]

### Design theory in a nutshell
In a study published in 2001,[^2] Del Ratzsch notes that several terms connected with design theory have not been properly defined.[^3] The design-theoretic way of thinking becomes much easier to understand once one learns its key terms:

The *intelligent design* (*genuine design*) of some observable object means that the object was brought about on purpose. Design is not the same thing as *optimal design*, which means the best possible implementation. An object that merely looks designed but is in fact the result of chance is not designed; in that case one speaks of *apparent design*.

Telling genuine design from apparent design is not always easy, and *design filters* are used to tell them apart. The work of recognizing design is called *inference to design*, and the study, development and application of such inferences is called *ID research*.

ID research on some object tests the *design hypothesis*, according to which the origin of that object is best explained by purpose, that is, by design. This testing can yield either a *positive result* that confirms the design hypothesis, i.e. a *design signal*, or a *negative result* that rejects it.

The central argument for *ID theory* is that, according to its proponents, ID research on certain objects taken from nature has given positive results, so that, in their view, at least this part of what natural science studies is reasonably regarded as designed on the strength of these design signals.

### What design theory is not
It would of course be easy to list a great many things that design theory is not. Here the focus is on correcting common but mistaken ideas about design theory.

#### Design theory is not the view that everything is optimally designed
On the basis of the Bible it is natural to think that everything observable is ultimately created by God and therefore intelligently – even perfectly intelligently, that is, optimally – designed. Such a view can even be said to be a logical consequence of [monotheistic](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoteismi) <!-- red link: monoteismi --> belief in creation.

Design theory, however, takes no stance in principle on [belief in creation](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kreationismi) <!-- red link: luomisusko --> or on monotheism; instead it uses its own terminology, independent of theology and based on the properties of observable objects and on the reasoning ability of the human mind. In it, the ***(intelligent) design*** (Engl. *intelligent design*) of some observable object means that at least one of the following two conditions holds:
1. the object was purposely made or caused (primary meaning);
1. the object's structure is purposive with respect to a certain function or pattern, and this function or pattern has no sufficiently credible explanation of its origin that is limited to known purposeless causes (secondary meaning).

That such a central concept has two meanings may seem odd, but it is explained by the way ID theory poses its question: since the origin of objects cannot be studied directly after the fact (nobody is building an already finished building any more), inferences are drawn from the properties of the objects (physics is enough to explain how a building stays up, but not how it could have come into being in the first place). If the method of inference is convincing enough, finding an object intelligently designed in the secondary sense means that it is not reasonable to assume that it was not intelligently designed in the primary sense as well. That is why these [intensionally](../glossary/sanasto.md#intensio) different meanings coincide [extensionally](../glossary/sanasto.md#ekstensio) in one and the same class of observable objects, and using different names for the same class of objects would not be helpful but would rather make the language unclear.

This can be compared to the fact that, biologically speaking, only the offspring of human beings are human beings. When we see, say, beings walking down the street whom we have never met before but who clearly look human, we still call them human on the strength of our impression of them alone, even though we have not seen them born of a human, have not checked whether their cells contain human DNA molecules, and have not inspected identity papers or birth certificates. It would not clarify matters but would on the contrary be awkward and frustrating if we were required to call human only those individuals for whose descent we have direct or indirect observational evidence or official documents, and to call others like us by some other name. ID-theoretic usage is in fact more precise than this ordinary usage, since a mere subjective impression is not enough for an inference to design: an object may be called designed only on the basis of closer study.

#### Design theory is not faulty argumentation
The argumentation in defense of design theory is often claimed to be faulty on the ground that design theory is supposedly not [falsifiable](../glossary/sanasto.md#falsifikaatiokriteeri) <!-- red link: falsifikaatiokriteeri -->. When one works according to design theory, an object of study can be found purposely caused, that is, designed, only on the basis of the published results of earlier research. Every design signal found is therefore open to criticism and thus falsifiable; it is enough to show that there are known "unintelligent", that is, "purposeless" causes (appealing to the observed regularity and/or randomness of natural phenomena) on which an explanatory model can be built that is credible enough as a [naturalistic explanation of the origin](../glossary/sanasto.md#naturalistinen-selittäminen) of the object in question. It is therefore wrong to claim that inference to design rests merely on personal incredulity (toward the theory of evolution), on [faith in authority](../articles/argumentum-ad-auctoritatem.md) (for instance in the doctrines of some religion), on an [appeal to ignorance](../articles/argumentum-ad-ignorantiam.md) or on the "[God of the gaps](../articles/aukkojen-jumala-v2.md)" fallacy.

A precisely formulated rebuttal of the latter accusations does, however, also require a critical look at these types of fallacy, since each of them exists not only in a generally valid logical form, which inference to design does not instantiate, but also in a <!--sanastoon tarvis saada käännös-->[degenerate](../glossary/sanasto.md#degeneroitua) [variant](../glossary/sanasto.md#variantti) based on naturalistic circular reasoning, which claims to recognize as a "fallacy" all argumentation that crosses the bounds of naturalism – and so, of course, also every design signal with its justification – even before, and without ever, looking into them.

A master's thesis of over 200 pages, approved in Finland in 2008, which examines the structure of the argumentation of one of the leading [ID theorists](../glossary/sanasto.md#id-teoreetikko), [Michael Behe](../articles/michael-behe.md),[^4] states the following about its conclusions on its abstract page: "The analysis shows that Behe succeeds in formulating his design inference so that it avoids much of the old criticism directed against the design argument. It further shows that what stand opposed in the debate over the idea of intelligent design are not only different conceptions of a philosophical argument but also worldviews and religious views." This research finding, based on a systematic analysis of the source material, is very far from the dismissive, rejecting attitude of the [extreme naturalists](../glossary/sanasto.md#äärinaturalismi).

#### Design theory is not a religious-political conspiracy aiming at theocracy
Even in forums generally considered reliable in Finland, the idea has been put forward that behind design theory there is some kind of conspiracy aimed at overthrowing Western democracy and setting up a theocracy ruled by religious leaders, which is supposedly being carried out by mixing religion and politics into science. Although the whole idea is ridiculous in its absurdity, how widespread it is probably gives reason enough to raise the subject.

To clear up the confusion, it is worth recalling such basic facts as that
1. all scientific research is conscious human activity,
1. all school teaching is likewise conscious human activity,
1. all conscious human activity has some basis of belief,
1. all research and teaching needs funding,
1. most of this funding comes from public funds, that is, mainly from taxes collected from the whole population,
1. all decision-making on the use of public funds is political in nature,
1. freedom of thought and of speech belong to Western democracy,
1. freedom of association and the right to political influence belong to Western democracy, and
1. the experience that the prevailing situation is unjust is the typical driving force of efforts at political change.
These generally known facts alone are enough to understand how things really stand:

When [Phillip Johnson](../pages/phillip-johnson.md) and certain others had concluded that neo-Darwinian evolutionism is an intellectually untenable (and, besides, socially harmful) basis of belief (point 3), which is nevertheless taught in all public schools as if it were proven certain (points 2, 3, 4 and 5), they felt the situation to be unjust and considered that it called for change (point 9). So they organized themselves around the [Discovery Institute](http://www.discovery.org) (point 8) in order to do research on these questions at least with private funds (points 1, 3 and 4) and to spread information on the matter (point 7), which in the best case could in time lead to the situation being put right (point 6). Since private fundraising cannot be justified the way public funding can (on the principle "You pay, or you cry and pay"), voluntary supporters also had to be told the purpose of the fundraising, and for this purpose an action plan addressed to supporters was drawn up. The opposing side, which holds that there is no alternative to evolutionism, obtained a copy of it, [possibly by illegal means](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedge-paperi), and it was then brought to public attention amid a great uproar, supposedly as evidence of some [conspiracy](../articles/kiila-strategia.md). The Discovery Institute long ago publicly explained [how things really stand](http://www.discovery.org/scripts/viewDB/filesDB-download.php?id=349), so those who keep the rumor mill turning would seem to have some aim other than factual reporting.

All in all, then, the whole uproar has no basis in fact other than this: by the lawful means a free Western society offers, people are trying to correct errors and one-sidedness in the practice of science and in public school teaching from the inside – through research and education, themselves part of the scientific community and the society whose shortcomings are to be corrected – and for this purpose voluntary donations are collected from those who agree that these aims are important. The other side, by contrast, uses all its [means of political pressure](http://www.aaas.org/news/press_room/evolution/) to prevent academically free ID research and balanced school teaching. These means include, for example, a [letter](http://www.aaas.org/news/releases/2008/media/0515ok_letter.pdf) to the governor of the state of Oklahoma seeking to overturn a law, passed in democratic order, that protects pupils' freedom of religion and opinion. From this example everyone can form their own view of how highly the circles trying to stir up suspicion of the ID movement themselves value Western democracy – both the freedoms on which it rests, such as freedom of speech and of religion, and its central expression, the legislative power of elected parliaments – when on the other side of the scales lies the absolute monopoly on the status of official school truth that the naturalistic explanation of the world has gained on questionable grounds. The repertoire of the extreme naturalists is, all in all, [varied](http://www.discovery.org/scripts/viewDB/index.php?command=view&id=2735), but what has been said will have to suffice here.

### The main design arguments

Design-theoretic work aims at a **paradigm shift** in science. This means a turning point at which a clear change takes place in the prevailing ways of thinking and acting, with the new taking the place of the old. For a paradigm shift to be possible, what is needed is new **questions**, new **concepts** with which these questions can be expressed, new **methods** with which these questions can be investigated, and new **research** done on this basis together with the new **results** it produces. The breakthrough succeeds only if resistance proves hopeless, that is, if the failure of the evolutionist model of thought and the superiority of the design-theoretic approach become so obvious that the change can no longer be fended off. Until then what is needed is **alternative funding** for research, **perseverance** in the work, **publication channels** for scientific exchange of ideas and for the results obtained, and a certain "bring it on" **attitude**, that is, a cheerful defiance in the face of the hostility, belittling and even outright persecution coming from the most zealous supporters of the ossified prevailing view. Paradigm shifts in science usually do not happen quickly; only when a new generation of researchers comes forward can new winds begin to blow.

Foundational design-theoretic work is unfinished but under way. This section presents the results of the work done so far.

#### Irreducible systems

*Main article: [Irreducible complexity](../articles/palautumaton-monimutkaisuus.md)*

*Main article: [The origin of complex systems](monimutkaisten-järjestelmien-synty.md)*

**[Michael Behe](../articles/michael-behe.md)**, in his book *[Darwin's Black Box](darwins-black-box-kirja.md)*, emphatically brought out the fact that the biochemical level of life[^5] depends for its functioning on countless different *molecular machines* made up of many parts, and that these machines typically work as wholes, so that removing any single part from such a machine breaks it so thoroughly that the output of the intact whole stops entirely, that is, the function the whole machine performs disappears.[^6] By Behe's clear logic, unguided processes working without purpose[^7] cannot get help from natural selection in finding such functions, because the selective advantages come only from finished machines.[^8] This in turn makes their probabilities of arising by chance infinitesimally small, and thereby the whole purposeless explanation of their origin absurdly implausible.[^9] Behe calls this all-or-nothing property **[irreducible complexity](../articles/palautumaton-monimutkaisuus.md)**[^10], and wholes that have this property, correspondingly, **irreducible systems**[^11]. Purposeless processes thus cannot by any means produce even a single such system in a *directly neo-Darwinian* way, and *this* fact is admitted even by Behe's knowledgeable naturalist critics.

The naturalist camp, which had earlier kept silent about the problem in public, has since had to try to answer this challenge Behe raised, that irreducible systems cannot be explained. It has done so by appealing, besides the demand for loyalty to naturalism as such,[^12] also to the possibilities of *indirect evolutionary routes*. In principle this means that the claim that "biological reality is just as neo-Darwinism requires" has in fact been abandoned, and there has been a retreat to the claim that "neo-Darwinism is[^13] the right explanation of biological reality, even though it certainly doesn't look like it".

In practice this move has meant resorting to various imaginary explanations according to which such molecular machines[^14] could have arisen by natural selection after all. This would supposedly have been possible if, for example,
1. the machine had earlier been even more complex than it is now but reducible[^15], from which state it then gradually "collapsed" into its present form,[^16] or if
1. a weaker precursor of the present function had originally been only a by-product of processes mainly serving some other function, whose existence then gave the precursors of the present system enough selective advantage to reach somehow a tolerable random step away from the present state, after which those other functions were no longer needed for selective advantage and could later have been pruned away.
Explanations of this kind are thus mere "hand-waving" without any actual evidence, and what makes things even worse for the naturalists is that no attempt is even made to support these scenarios with credible probability calculations<!--  *[lähde puuttuu]* – Keskustelusivulla kohta <4> -->; it is pure "we might perhaps imagine" talk, of a kind that within evolutionism people have grown so used to over the years that it now causes surprise when not everyone swallows it whole any more. It is, however, a purely [mathematical fact](tarkoituksellisuuspäättelyn-logiikka.md) that in a world of finite resources like ours not every nice thing can have happened – that is, had time to happen – purposelessly and by chance, and purposelessness that needs a miracle to help it is already starting to be purposefulness in disguise.

The paradox, then, is that it is precisely the naturalists, who oppose miracles body and soul, who would themselves need quite a few miracles to make their purposeless model work,[^17] whereas the (where needed) supernaturalist intelligent design, that is, the theory of purposeful causation, does **not** need miracles to help it – any more than I need a "miracle" to get the string of characters I want produced from a keyboard.[^18]

#### Double CCC
The effectiveness of the mutation–natural selection mechanism can be assessed quantitatively. There are two ways to do this that carry evidential weight in natural science:
1. relying on the generally available empirical data on the biosphere, and
1. publicly presenting mathematical calculations based on research data on the possibilities of change at the cellular level.
The two ways support each other and point consistently to the conclusion that the neo-Darwinian (or any other unguided) mechanism cannot possibly be the explanation of the origin of all observable biodiversity.

In technical treatments of the subject, the probability of chloroquine resistance arising by chance, which is 10<sup>-20</sup>, can be used as the unit for the complexity of attainable advantages (resistance to the drug chloroquine arises by chance in about one in 10<sup>20</sup> malaria parasites, which, given the immense total number of malaria parasites, is still just barely possible). From the English expression *chloroquine-complexity cluster* we get the symbol of the unit for this degree of improbability (CCC). The requirement that two random changes this improbable occur at the same time, i.e. the [double CCC](../articles/kaksois-ccc.md), is then with good reason a quantitative limit that chance-origin mechanisms cannot exceed. Explanations based on them should therefore never assume that mutations this demanding have happened. For the number of living cells there have been in natural history is not enough to make credible the assumption that even a single biological novelty this demanding has ever arisen by unguided chance alone.

Nor can natural selection help chance when the novelty yields no selective advantage before it is finally complete. It is precisely random mutations that are supposed to be the engine of the evolutionary mechanism, and so a clear limit to the power of this "engine" has been shown, one it cannot exceed.

#### Specified complexity
Specified complexity is a model of inference, developed by the mathematician, philosopher and theologian William Dembski,[^19] by which design can be detected scientifically [^20].

Specified complexity is a key concept that describes how meaningful information differs, on the one hand, from simple law-like ordered structures such as crystals and snowflakes, and on the other hand from completely random sequences, which are improbable but mean nothing. It is precisely this concept that sums up one essential difference between biological structures (and the information behind them) and inanimate nature. The term has been used independently of Dembski by, for example, the physicist Paul Davies and several biologists. The physicist Paul Davies has said that Dembski's work contains several really useful ideas.

*Image: W. Dembski's design filter as a flowchart.*

In Dembski's model, design is reached through an eliminative process with three main steps:
1. Contingency (degrees of freedom)
1. Complexity
1. Specification
The form of the inference is also shown in the accompanying diagram.

Besides the three steps above, Dembski considers both the resources available and the number of possible specifications.

We can explain the thing under examination by natural law, by chance or by design. For us to conclude that the thing is designed, it must be contingent, complex and specified. Below we go through these steps at the level of the idea.

Following the diagram, we begin by asking: were there degrees of freedom in the situation? In other words, did the thing happen of necessity, or could something else have happened too? If the event happened of necessity, like objects falling under gravity or water freezing into ice below zero, natural law (necessity) is the best explanation.

If the event was contingent, we ask the next question: is the thing to be explained complex? Is the probability of its arising by chance small? If the probability of the observed thing is fairly large, it is best explained by chance. For example, the probability of the outcome of two coin tosses is 1/2*1/2, and that of a die roll 1/6. Such an event happening is not surprising, so we explain it by chance.

If the event was improbable enough, we ask the last question: can the thing, observation or event be specified independently? Can we describe the thing or event independently of it? This criterion is needed because even if the event was improbable (complex), something had to happen anyway. For example, 1000 coin tosses in a row necessarily give some outcome, and all outcomes are equally probable. If we cannot specify or describe the event or thing independently of it, we explain it by chance.

If we can specify or describe the event independently of the outcome, for example "all tails", we can conclude that it is not chance but design – say, a coin someone has weighted.

For example, the text of the novel Tuntematon Sotilas is not determined by natural law. The natural laws acting on paper and ink do not determine what the book says. (Information rides on matter.) The text is therefore contingent. The probability of the whole novel arising by chance is astronomically small, in fact so small that it is no longer reasonable to explain the novel's origin as a statistical fluke. It is therefore complex. A random generator would also have produced some string of letters, but the novel is also specified: it is clear Finnish and, what is more, gives a brilliant portrayal of war. Since the novel is contingent, complex and specified, no explanation of its origin based on natural law or chance is known. Intelligence, by contrast, is known to be able to produce large amounts of specified complex information, so by analogy it can be concluded that the novel is the result of intelligent design. The example is simple but illustrates the explanatory filter. The same reasoning can also be applied to things found in nature.

Dembski's design inference can also be presented in [Bayesian form](../articles/bayesin-teoreema.md). A fuller treatment of Dembski's filter is also available in Finnish [^21].

#### A fine-tuned universe
As the natural sciences have advanced, it has had to be acknowledged that at the various levels of size and structure of material reality there are [fine-tuning phenomena](kosminen-hienosäätö.md) that have no [naturalistic explanation](../glossary/sanasto.md#naturalistinen-selittäminen) but that are necessary in the teleological sense for our observable world to make possible our own existence and, among other things, our scientific research. That is why it is precisely the assumption of purposeful causation that explains these phenomena naturally.

### Is design theory a new form of creationism?

*Main article: [Design theory and creationism](../articles/suunnitteluteoria-ja-kreationismi.md)*

Design theory is often criticized for things that belong to creationism.[^22] The answer to the question whether design theory is a form of creationism depends on the definitions of creationism and design theory being used.[^23] By the definitions used in ApoWiki, design theory is not [creationism](../glossary/sanasto.md#kreationismi), because creationism argues from a divinely revealed text that living things are created. Creationism also takes a stance on, for example, the time of creation and the identity or motives of the creator. Design theory, for its part, argues from empirical observations of the properties of living things or of the cosmos that some kind of intelligent designer is the best explanation for how the observed [specified complexity](../glossary/sanasto.md#täsmennetty-monimutkaisuus) <!-- red link: täsmennetty monimutkaisuus --> came about. Design theory takes no stance on the identity of the designer for the simple reason that it is not possible, on the basis of design theory, to prove that the designer is the God of the Bible.[^24] Creationism is generally considered to fit within the concept of design theory, but design theory is generally not considered to fit within the concept of creationism. They are therefore two different things – just like Christianity and Catholicism. According to [Behe](../articles/michael-behe.md), supporting design theory follows from "the facts – not from sacred books or the beliefs of some sect."[^25]

#### Background and outside views
The question of how design theory relates to creationism has become socially significant especially in the United States, where scientific creationism has been classified by a court ruling as religion in the legal sense that it may not be taught in publicly maintained schools. The question also matters because, in the view of supporters of the so-called scientific worldview, religion is by definition the opposite of science, and if design theory is creationism and creationism is religion, then design theory too is religion and therefore non-science and untrue. Since this outlook also includes [naturalism](../glossary/sanasto.md#naturalismi) (the material world is held to be self-sufficient, so that nothing outside it can in any way act on it), even the idea that there could be an intelligence outside the world which designs things within the world and carries out its designs in the world, and the proposal of a research program treating this possibility as one alternative, are regarded as an anti-scientific neo-creationist conspiracy. For these reasons people actively avoid even the name design theory (or "intelligent design theory") and prefer to speak of, for example, ID creationism.

Established creationist organizations, for their part, tend to see design theory as a failed compromise between genuine biblical-apologetic creationism and the prevailing anti-Christian university science: too Christian-minded to gain significant ground in a scientific community mostly committed to naturalism, yet too vague to help people to saving Christian faith either. Since creationist outlooks have long been divided into subtypes (mainly by their views on the age of creation), design theory may in these circles be classified as a subclass of creationism of its own, likewise called ID creationism, which is then considered a view headed in the right direction but insufficient, and is therefore opposed mainly on theological grounds.

This legal and worldview baggage is part of what makes the relationship between design theory and creationism awkward to discuss. When one sees design theory treated as creationism, it is therefore good to find out who is making that claim, what they are aiming at with it and what definitions of the concepts they use. After that one no longer needs to trip over words but can see the substantive questions behind them.

#### Design theorists' own understanding
According to the biologist Paul Nelson, design theory is a minimal commitment to detecting intelligent causation.[^26]
According to [William Dembski](william-dembski.md), who has done pioneering work in ID theory, design theory is a scientific theory with no religious commitments[^27]. Unlike creationism, design theory takes no prior stance on questions such as *Who is the designer?* or *How does the designer act when designing and building things?*, because the nature, moral qualities and aims of this intelligence lie outside the domain of natural science. The designer is compatible with the Creator God of the world's great monotheistic religions ([Judaism](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juutalaisuus) <!-- red link: juutalaisuus -->, [Christianity](../articles/kristinusko.md) and [Islam](../articles/islam.md)), and likewise with the watchmaker God of deism, the demiurge of Plato's *Timaeus* and the divine reason of the Stoics (*logos spermatikos*) [^28].

This view thus approaches the question in the context of the history and philosophy of science: at different times the outlook of the scientific community has been dominated, and the work of its mainstream of the day guided, by various currents of opinion, caused partly by the prevailing views of each era and partly by the scientific discoveries made up to that time and their interpretations. From time to time there have been major shifts of outlook in the natural sciences. [Design theorists](../glossary/sanasto.md#suunnitteluteoreetikko) hold that the best possible explanation of the scientific observations available precisely in our own time may require assuming a real purposeful cause, and that these questions can be handled by the logico-mathematical methods typical of natural science. In developing and applying such methods they do not regard themselves as representing aims hostile to science but, on the contrary, as forces of change within the scientific community that are necessary to make science's self-correcting ability possible.

Science always also has personal significance for those who practice it. Many (though not all) of the leading names in design theory are active Christians (of various denominations). Likewise, many (though not all) of the leading advocates of the theory of evolution are active atheists. Of course everyone would prefer to see the mainstream of science agree with their own personal conviction. Insofar as science is about more than mere power politics, however, each view put forward as the best explanation of observed phenomena should be examined in relation to the observed phenomena themselves, not in relation to the so-called rules of the game of science laid down by some science-policy pressure group. The rules of the game of science can change and have changed before; observational results confirmed by repeated experiments, by contrast, are the same for everyone, and the search for their best explanation, and the case for its being the best, succeed the better, the less ideological baggage researchers are required to carry.

#### On the demarcation question
Intelligible discussion is also hampered by the fact that design theory's claim that design can be verified without naming the designer is often interpreted **exclusively** in public discussion conducted by outsiders, as though it were some separate, all-embracing way of thinking that would include, among other things, refusing to take a personal stance on, for example, the age of the earth or the identity of the Designer. This, however, is a mistaken interpretation. Design theory confines itself to the natural-scientific questions about explaining the origin of observed objects, and so, as a **[research program](../articles/tutkimusohjelma.md)**, it contains no stance on, for example, questions of dating or of the salvation of souls. This does not mean, however, that it forbids its supporters to form personal convictions on these matters, to belong to communities that take such positions, or to state these views publicly. These views simply fall outside the questions design theory has marked out as its own object of study, and agreements or disagreements about them are therefore not part of the discussion about **design theory**.

Toward positions taken in other contexts, by contrast, the ID research community is **inclusive**: it accepts as full members those who think differently about them and, if they wish, discuss them publicly, without demanding any self-censorship of them. Thus, for example, [Michael Behe](../articles/michael-behe.md) still holds to [common descent](../glossary/sanasto.md#polveutumisoppi), whereas another quite well-known design theorist, [Paul Nelson](../pages/paul-nelson.md), is a public proponent of [young-earth creationism](../glossary/sanasto.md#yec-näkemys), and this state of affairs poses no problem and no need for secrecy as far as ID research is concerned. The atheist philosopher Dr [Bradley Monton](../articles/bradley-monton.md) and the agnostic [David Berlinski](../pages/david-berlinski.md) are also advocates of ID[^29].

### Science or not?
The question whether design theory is scientific can be divided into three sub-questions:
1. Can design theory be science?
1. If it can, is it science or not?
1. If it is, is it valid and therefore acceptable science, or faulty and therefore to be rejected?

#### Can design theory be science?
Is science, or at least natural science, compatible with the possibility of [supernaturalist](../glossary/sanasto.md#supranaturalismi) explanations or not? This [philosophy-of-science](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tieteenfilosofia) <!-- red link: tieteenfilosofia --> question seeks the limits and the rules of the game of science. Logically considered, the question is then about the postulates of doing science, that is, those fundamental starting points that cannot even in principle be proved scientifically but that must nevertheless be assumed true, or valid in some other sense, for science to be done at all.

This question is contested at bottom simply because scientists, now as before, fundamentally disagree with one another about it on a personal level and are privately committed to different postulates. So some of them are firmly of the opinion that research must be [naturalistic](../glossary/sanasto.md#naturalismi) in order to be scientific at all. Others, again, have publicly disowned such a restriction.[http://www.dissentfromdarwin.org](http://www.dissentfromdarwin.org) Many have perhaps never even stopped to think about their own work at the level of its philosophical foundations.

The key points of this question concern
1. research from what starting points can get funding from public funds,
1. research from what starting points may be presented in academic publication series,
1. research from what starting points must be taken into account when assessing a researcher's academic qualifications,
1. the results of research from what starting points are fit to be taught in publicly funded schools, and
1. research from what starting points may be presented as science in documentaries and science articles aimed at the general public.

As is easy to see, all these questions belong to the power politics within the scientific community, or of society at large, rather than to scientific research. They cannot therefore be settled by scientific research; the question is which view's supporters manage, at a given stage of history, to make their own view prevail in society and/or in the scientific community. In this sense it is justified to speak of an ongoing culture war in which there is no impartial or neutral standpoint, though there are certainly more tolerant and less tolerant attitudes.

At the level of logical analysis it should be noted that insofar as science is **defined** as naturalistic, it can take no stance on supernaturalism except as regards its own internal workings. A logical naturalistic science therefore cannot claim, for example, that there is no reason to believe in the supernatural, since however many and however weighty such reasons there might be, commitment to naturalism has from the outset blinded science to them. Illogical thinking, for its part, can claim anything at all, but it can hardly be considered scientific.

#### If design theory can be science, is it?
Accepting the possibility in principle of a supernaturalist scientific explanation is thus a logically necessary condition for comparing the relative merits of supernaturalist and naturalistic explanations scientifically. This does not mean, however, that just any supernaturalist explanation would be accepted as scientific. A scientific explanation can be required to have, for example, internal consistency, reliance on grounds that are in principle observable by everyone, and [falsifiability](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falsifiointi) <!-- red link: falsifioitavuus -->.

Insofar as design theory is assessed as a whole, it quite evidently seeks to meet the criteria above and thus **seeks to be** science in the sense they intend. Strictly speaking, however, these criteria cannot be applied to a research program as such, but only to the individual studies published, or submitted for publication, within it. A closer examination of these is therefore a logical necessity if one is to give a well-founded answer to the question whether an individual study is scientific.

So when one sees publications by supporters of design theory being called unscientific, it pays to note what points the concrete criticism concerns in each case: is the critic ultimately saying that this research **cannot** be science, or is he admitting that by its **starting points** it could be science but that the **way it is carried out** shows it to be unscientific? This is not always easy to make out, however, since the criticism is not necessarily well articulated; its aim may rather be simply to warn the public about design theory without offering arguments that would withstand closer scrutiny in support of the warning. It may also be an invalid attempt at argument, [vitiated by a fallacy](../articles/argumentointivirheet.md). (The invalidity of an argument does not in itself prove the claim false, but faulty reasoning gives the claim no support either, so it remains a mere claim among claims.)

#### If design theory is science, is it valid or faulty science?
The previous sections have dealt with certain basic criteria of science, some contested (naturalism) and some uncontested (verifiability, consistency, falsifiability). This section is about discussion within science: a view may in itself meet the criteria of science (defined in some way) and in that sense be scientific, and yet be so clearly less successful than competing scientific views that it should therefore be rejected – **after** its failure has been shown beyond reasonable dispute. Such a rejection within science is thus logically possible only if the theory has first been given the chance to show its usefulness in the internal processes of science: in research work done with public funding too, and in debate conducted in academic forums.

Because of the strong rejection, chiefly from [extreme naturalists](../glossary/sanasto.md#äärinaturalismi), the validity of design theory has not yet been put to the test in this way. It is interesting in itself that the extreme naturalists, precisely by opposing the possibility that design theory **could** be scientific, prevent the alternative that it might have to be rejected because it is **faulty**.

### The explanatory value of design theory
#### The naturalist view of the explanatory value of design theory
The question of the explanatory value of design theory is closely bound up with the question of its scientific status. According to the [naturalistic](../glossary/sanasto.md#naturalismi) view, only explanations based on natural causes can count as scientific, so ID theory cannot have any explanatory value. For conceptual clarity it is worth distinguishing, on the one hand, the philosophical-methodological position of [methodological naturalism](../glossary/sanasto.md#metodologinen-naturalismi) as such and, on the other, the effort to ram it into a monopoly position in science policy. It is possible to be a methodological naturalist and seek to strengthen support for this view purely by means internal to science (research, teaching, taking part in debates and other public discussion); by contrast, the coercive policy that stops at nothing to secure a monopoly for publicly funded evolutionist research, communication and school teaching – a policy based on the atheist so-called scientific world picture adopted by the leadership of many associations nominally founded to promote science and its teaching, or civil liberties in general – is an example of [extreme naturalist](../glossary/sanasto.md#äärinaturalismi) activism.

This demand for ideological dictatorship is justified with somewhat varying arguments, whose common underlying idea can be taken to be the (conscious or unconscious) view that the task of science is to give atheists a [feeling of intellectual fulfillment](http://www.positiveatheism.org/hist/quotes/dawkins.htm), based on the impression that any object of study whatever can be handled in a natural-seeming way without having to assume anything supernatural. Few, admittedly, state this aim as bluntly as Richard Lewontin, who has frankly admitted that the materialist approach to research does not follow from observations but is a conscious philosophical choice that determines what is [considered acceptable](http://wiki.cotch.net/index.php/Lewontin_on_materialism) in a naturalistically constructed science. Insofar as science has at some point failed to achieve this aim of furthering atheists' peace of mind, it is urged to try even harder in the future.

The goal of extreme naturalist science policy is to define science officially and once and for all as the effort to find the [best **materialistic** explanation](http://www.leaderu.com/science/heresytrial.html) for observed phenomena. In this way free inquiry in the philosophy of science, continual reflection, and mutually critical discussion between fundamentally different views based on verifiable arguments would be replaced by a naturalistic thought police appealing to legal norms of various levels, which would prevent in advance any research into the foundations of science carried out on the terms of an [open space of discussion](../glossary/sanasto.md#avoin-keskusteluavaruus), as well as the higher education and public science-policy discussion based on such research.

#### On the nature of design-theoretic explanation
The approach of design theory can be regarded as a genuine extension of [methodological naturalism](../glossary/sanasto.md#metodologinen-naturalismi): natural explanations are given priority but not a monopoly.  So if the regularities observed in nature (the so-called laws of nature) or randomness suffice to explain an observed phenomenon, no other explanation need be sought. There is, however, a third acceptable kind of explanation as well, one used routinely in science in other contexts (for example in criminal investigation): purposeful causation.

*Image: The journal Science named the reprogramming of cells the most important news of 2008[^30]. How can something be reprogrammed if it was never programmed in the first place? This raises the question whether blind and unguided processes are able to program anything[^31].*

Naturalism rules out the purposeful alternative in advance whenever the possible purposeful cause would apparently have to be supernatural in nature. This "supernaturalness", however, means something different in naturalist ideology than in everyday language, since even the real influence of the capacities of human consciousness (acts of will) on events in the material world is, from the standpoint of consistent naturalism, an unscientific [supernaturalist](../glossary/sanasto.md#supranaturalismi) view that should in time be replaced by a "genuinely scientific" explanation based on purposeless processes and physical effects. Consistent naturalism would therefore rule out an ultimately purposeful explanation in every case (criminal investigation included), since it is never a "natural explanation" in the naturalist sense. But since raising this point in public discussion would improve neither the credibility nor the chances of success of the (extreme) naturalists, in their propaganda against ID theory they prefer to equate the design-theoretic purposeful alternative with belief in fairies or goblins rather than with the intuitively natural view that matches everyday experience: that there obviously exist conscious beings who can carry out purposes they consider good by acting on the material world, and that we can know this easily and reliably, because we are such beings ourselves.

Unlike naturalism, then, design theory regards purposeful causation, at the level of principle, as an entirely legitimate alternative for scientific explanation too, and puts this basic view into practice by developing and using methods whose aim is to verify purposefulness reliably in sufficiently clear cases (and only in them), regardless of what other properties the cause of phenomena that turn out to have been purposely brought about must be thought to have in each case. At the core of these methods are the principled admission, and the practical mathematical assessment, that explanations relying on purposelessness have limited explanatory power, together with measuring how "hard to cause" the phenomena under study are. What probably most threatens the feeling of intellectual fulfillment of atheists who have learned to believe in the unlimited explanatory power of natural explanations is precisely that ID research, by its mere existence, refutes the assumption that no reasonable and workable alternative to naturalism, in the philosophy of science and in practical research method, can be put forward.

#### General criteria for assessing explanatory value
In assessing the explanatory value of design theory, it is again worth distinguishing the question whether such research **may be done in science at all** (that is, whether it can be science) from the question of what is to be said about the research done or planned and its results, if their scientific status is not denied in advance on an ideological-philosophical-science-policy level; in that case one has to assess, on the one hand, **how convincing the foundations of design-theoretic methodology are** and, on the other, **each study carried out on that basis separately**, as is generally done in science.

The starting point for developing design-theoretic methodology is **the aim of preventing false positives**: what is regarded as the problem is not classifying designed objects as having arisen without purpose but classifying objects that (even possibly) arose without purpose as designed. Design theory thus resembles [methodological naturalism](../glossary/sanasto.md#metodologinen-naturalismi) in that both share **the aim of minimizing the number of objects classified as designed** – the only difference being that the methodological naturalist gets through this "job" simply by declaring himself a methodological naturalist who, in his role as researcher, cannot even in principle regard his objects of study as purposely caused, whereas the design theorist has to take pains to get his objects of study classified in this respect. (This difference somewhat resembles the disagreement in the philosophy of mathematics between Platonists and constructivists: constructivists have to take pains to construct the mathematical objects of study they regard as adequate, which on the Platonist view already exist and can therefore also be meaningfully referred to.) Another essential difference, of course, is that by this declarative approach the methodological naturalist "manages" to reach the extreme result in this minimization effort (the empty set), whereas design theorists have had to admit that the field of natural science also contains objects that cannot meaningfully be classified as having arisen without purpose within the regularities known to science as it now stands and the probabilistic resources <!-- red link: probabilistiset resurssit --> afforded by the prevailing Big Bang cosmology. – Each reader can judge for themselves which of these methodological alternatives does better at making possible the qualities required or hoped for in science.

### The apologetic value of design theory

#### Design theory and the teleological argument
Seen from the side of apologetics, design theory appears so closely related to the classical [teleological argument](teleologinen-argumentti.md) that it has even been regarded as a [variant](../glossary/sanasto.md#variantti) of it. Looked at more closely, or from more than one direction, essential differences are nevertheless easy to see:
* The teleological argument is cosmogonic in character, concerned with the origin of the world, whereas design-theoretic methods apply just as well to recognizing the work of designers within the world.
* Behind the teleological argument lies the idea of optimal design; in design theory it is enough to show that design is real – its possible optimality is, in that sense, an extra, additional property.
* The teleological argument does not, as design theory does, divide causes into regular, (unguided) random and intelligent ones. Cosmogony is a fundamental question in the sense that the answer given to it also provides the starting point for building a methodology for further research: if [theism](../glossary/sanasto.md#teismi) is the right understanding of the world, regularities or random events cannot be ultimate explanations; behind them, too, is the action of a personal God. The design-theoretic approach to research, by contrast, is a kind of genuine extension of methodological naturalism, so that in the design-theoretic method purposefulness, that is, genuine design, is defined as an alternative to regularity and randomness, not as the ultimate nature of a creation dependent on God, (possibly) hidden even behind them.
* The teleological argument is by nature an argument belonging specifically to theological apologetics, whereas design theory is an alternative approach belonging to the natural sciences.
Despite these differences, it is quite right to say that design theory can give the teleological argument fresh topicality and added weight, and that this is one reason to pay apologetic attention to it. The "division of labor" then runs roughly like this: design theory can serve as a "subcontractor" to the teleological argument by showing that, when things are examined honestly, [methodological naturalism](../glossary/sanasto.md#metodologinen-naturalismi) is not able to nullify the teleological argument; [naturalistic](../glossary/sanasto.md#naturalismi) wishful thinking, and the research and teaching based on it, thus do not succeed in nullifying the reality of the teleology observable in creation – on the contrary, that teleology breaks down the credibility of the naturalistic outlook, and perhaps it is precisely design theory that can best open people's eyes to see this.

#### The apologetic value of design theory with regard to non-Christians
Design theory thus does not aim, and in principle could not, to prove that it was precisely the God who revealed himself to mankind in words, in the writings collected as the Bible, who originally created the world. In that sense one can say that it has no Christian apologetic value with regard to, for example, Muslims. Nor does it aim, nor could it, to show that the world of the senses is not mere illusion, a view held at least by Hinduism. With regard to materialists and naturalists, however, it can have apologetic value insofar as they are willing to break away from their own mantras and bellwethers in order to look into things for themselves. It may then turn out that the foundations on which their understanding of reality has so far been built are rather flimsy, and this can open their minds to hear also what can be put forward, at the level of understanding, specifically on behalf of Christianity.

#### The apologetic value of design theory for the Christian's own faith
Design theory can also be useful for the Christian's own faith. For despite claims to the contrary, Christianity is not at bottom a [fideist](../glossary/sanasto.md#fideismi) way of thinking that would value believing for the sake of believing. The Bible, on the contrary, both gives rational grounds for its own reliability (e.g.  ([Luke 1:1](../books/luk.md))–4;  ([1 Cor. 15:1](../books/1co.md))–8;  ([2 Pet. 1:16](../books/2pe.md))–19;  ([1 John 1:1](../books/1jn.md))–3) and in many places also presupposes that God has given reliable revelation of himself in non-verbal form as well, and that this testimony of God's works must be taken seriously. So the Bible and design-theoretic study of nature hold the same basic view: that it is logically entirely possible to come to know something about God by observing his works ( ([Rom. 1:19](../books/rom.md))–20), and that the matter is therefore worth serious study. This shared basic view runs counter not only to the prevailing background ideology of natural science but to just the same degree to the prevailing trend in academic theology. That too is why it is not surprising that [many church leaders](../articles/liberaali-kristinusko.md) also find the whole ID debate embarrassing.
