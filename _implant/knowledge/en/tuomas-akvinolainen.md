# Thomas Aquinas

*Topics: [People](../topics/henkilöt.md), [Philosophy](../topics/filosofia.md), [Theism](../topics/teismi.md).*

**Thomas Aquinas** (1225-1274)[^1] was a Catholic theologian and apologist. He is known above all for his five proofs of God.

### The five proofs of God
#### The unmoved mover
According to Aquinas, nothing moves unless it is moved. One could object, though, that a human being, for example, can move himself by walking. The objection needs a closer look: a walking human being is moved by his muscles, which are moved by the body's reactions, and these need small-scale motion in order to work. The series of motions can be traced back to a first mover. According to Aquinas, the series of movers cannot go on to infinity, because without a first mover there would be no other movers either, and so "Therefore it is necessary to arrive at a first mover, moved by no other; and this everyone understands to be God."[^2]

#### The first cause
This argument repeats the pattern of the previous one in a different formulation. In practice it is the same argument, with the moved and the mover replaced by cause and effect.[^3]

#### The necessary being
According to Aquinas, non-necessary beings can either be or not be. He thinks that if no necessary being exists, then there has been and will be a state in which nothing exists. If there has been such a state, the present state of affairs is not possible. Aquinas thinks that this necessary being is God.[^4]

#### The argument from perfection
According to this argument, the differences of degree between things require a perfect, objective standard for them. Thomas seems to think that differences of degree in properties, or levels of perfection, derive from the highest level, that is, from God. Here Aquinas's thinking resembles Plato's thinking about the world of Ideas.[^5]
