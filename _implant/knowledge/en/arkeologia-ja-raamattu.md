# Archaeology and the Bible

*Topics: [History](../topics/historia.md), [Bible](../topics/raamattu.md).*

1. Bible critics questioned the existence of David. Since then, two archaeological mentions of David have been found (Tel Dan, Mesha).
1. The ossuary of the high priest Caiaphas has been found.
1. Even the existence of Pilate was doubted, but then his name was found on a stone slab.
1. In skeletal remains we see details corresponding to the crucifixion account.
1. The sea voyage of Luke and Paul accurately describes the sea conditions of the time and the wind conditions of the region.
1. In countless places the Bible has exposed itself to verification. When one passage is confirmed, critics merely shift to questioning some other passage.

Theologian and archaeologist Eero Junkkaala on the subject:

> So, what does the Bible look like in the light of this research?
> The overall picture is completely clear: we are dealing with a historical book.
> The names of fifteen kings of Israel and Judah are also found in texts outside the Bible. A mudbrick gate from Abraham's time has been excavated at Dan, and the gates built by Solomon at Hazor, Megiddo, and Gezer, which fits extremely well with the mention of Solomon in 1 Kings 9:15. The altar built by King Jeroboam has been found, and the ruins of a palace from the time of King Ahab have been excavated in Samaria. From them, an exceptionally large number of ivory objects were found, which fits remarkably well with the description in Amos 6:4… Thus, the Bible is part of the real history of the Near East.
