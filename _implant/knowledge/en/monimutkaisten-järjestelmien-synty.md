# The origin of complex systems

*Topics: [Design theory](../topics/suunnitteluteoria.md), [Theory of evolution](../topics/evoluutioteoria.md).*


**The origin of complex systems** is undoubtedly one of the greatest challenges to the [theory of evolution](evoluutioteoria.md). The storage and processing of information in living beings, self-regulation, response to changes caused by the environment, and replication are based on complex biochemical wholes. Whether unguided and purposeless evolutionary processes are able to produce these systems came into wider discussion with the book by American biochemist [Michael Behe](../articles/michael-behe.md), [Darwin's Black Box](darwins-black-box-kirja.md). In the book, published in 1996, Behe argues that many biochemical systems require the simultaneous presence of several different parts in order to function. This poses a challenge to Darwinian processes, according to which organisms develop through small intermediate steps rather than by large leaps. Since multi-part systems lose their function when their basic parts are missing, evolutionary processes that advance in small steps cannot provide a selective advantage, because the function is reached only when all the necessary parts are present. Therefore the theory of evolution cannot provide a credible explanation for the origin of these systems.
Although the great majority of the community of biologists rejects Behe's argument, for all its self-confidence it has not been able to offer biochemically detailed explanations or testable models that would solve the problems. The purpose of this article is to examine models of the origin of various biochemical and cell-biological systems.

Humans have built many complex systems.[^1] Nature, however, contains far more complex systems[^2], which humans did not originally bring about and which they have not even been able to produce afterwards from non-living raw materials.[^3] How did they come into being? What kinds of causal factors (efficient causes) does the origin of complex systems require?

### The theory of evolution as a metaphysics-driven research program

[Design theorist](suunnitteluteoria.md) William Dembski has drawn attention to the similarities between alchemy and the theory of evolution[^4]: the alchemists' confidence that lead could be turned into gold by the means at their disposal[^5] rested on their metaphysical worldview, namely the [Neoplatonic](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uusplatonismi) idea that lead, as a base metal, was in any case striving toward becoming a precious metal – that is, was about to turn into gold quite naturally. Dembski points out an essential similarity between alchemists and Darwinists: each is so spellbound by his own metaphysical commitment that the harsh results of experience hardly move him.

#### Even alchemy had advantages over the theory of evolution

In one sense the Darwinist is in an even worse position than the alchemist: the alchemist was at least free to use all his imagination and ingenuity in his pursuits,[^6] whereas [physicalism](fysikalismi.md)[^7] puts its adherents in an intellectual straitjacket. Explanatory models for the origin of life, of the various forms of life, of consciousness or of any object of study that are built on terms other than those of physicalism may not even be considered, neither by him nor – insofar as it is up to him – by anyone else within his sphere of influence.

Another essential difference between alchemists and Darwinists is that the alchemists honestly admitted their repeated failures: despite their attempts, no gold had been produced, and the "philosopher's stone" was still to be found.[^8] Darwinists, by contrast, are committed to a stronger claim than the alchemists: evolution is supposed to happen all the time, entirely on its own.[^9] So no failure can be admitted, when the whole biosphere supposedly proves the theory of evolution a fact by its mere existence.[^10]

#### The Achilles' heel of the theory of evolution: the complete lack of causal specifications

A clear characteristic shared by alchemists and Darwinists is that both are unshakeably convinced they know that the things their worldview requires can happen, even though they cannot work out in detail which conditions make them happen.

* Hypotheses or theories about a particular phenomenon can be said to contain a *causal specification* of that phenomenon if and only if they can describe (with the precision that practical testing requires) the factors needed to bring that phenomenon about (that is, give at least some sufficient condition for its occurrence). This does not require knowing *how* exactly the phenomenon arises under those conditions; it is enough to know *that* it arises under them[^11]. A theory that contains no causal specification of the phenomenon it deals with cannot be tested in the way empirical natural science requires, and so cannot be falsified either.

* A theory that contains a causal specification can be said to be *causally specifying*, and one of its properties is then *causal specificity*.
* If observations confirm a causal specification of some phenomenon contained in a theory, that phenomenon can be said to be *causally specified*.
* Within methodological naturalism, both the [origin of biological life](../articles/abiogeneesi.md) and **all** of its essential phenomena and structures are *not causally specified*.[^12]

#### Ways of propping up the facade of the theory of evolution

In practice, then, the Darwinists' toolkit has been reduced[^13] mainly to announcing, loudly and from on high, conclusions that go far beyond the observations, and to marketing them grandly. The experimental results and other observations behind this advertising barrage are in themselves quite modest compared with what Darwinism needs, and on closer inspection they often even support [design theory](suunnitteluteoria.md) rather than Darwinism.[^14]

"Embedding" observed phenomena in the metaphysical framework of Darwinism by means of development (frame) stories and terminology has so far been perhaps the most central way of hiding the problems of the theory of evolution. For example, calling the variation, adaptation and speciation observed in the objects of biological study "evolution" is ultimately a mere rhetorical trick to make the theory of evolution more credible.[^15]

### A step toward testability: computer simulation of evolution

Since the experimental results obtained in biology leave a great deal to be desired from Darwinism's point of view, attempts have been made to help the situation by writing and promoting computer programs whose operation is claimed to demonstrate directly the creative powers of evolutionary mechanisms. Since they are promoted as counterparts of biological evolution and as demonstrations of its credibility, they too fall, to that extent, within the subject of this article.

#### Avida

*Avida* is artificial-life simulation software intended for research in evolutionary biology. Developed by the *MSU Digital evolution lab*, it is undoubtedly the most hyped software of its kind. According to philosopher Robert Pennock, a member of the Avida development team, *Avida does not simulate evolution but is an instance of it*. [^16] Richard Lenski's research group has used it to try to show that complex biological systems can develop by a Darwinian mechanism. A critical examination of the results can be found [here](http://www.arn.org/blogs/index.php/2/2008/08/09/avida_as_a_teleo_logic_model_of_life).

To explain credibly how complex biological systems can develop by Darwinian mechanisms, however, Avida relies on unrealistic starting assumptions:[^17][^18]

1. The genomes of the organisms are assumed to be much smaller than what is actually necessary.
   * The ratio of the necessary genome to the whole genome is 15/50 at the start. Filling the genome with worthless genetic material is compensated with extra metabolic energy, instead of the organism being harmed by it as in nature.
1. Mutation rates are extremely high relative to genome size.
1. Functions critical for survival are hard to destroy by mutation.
1. New and more complex functions are assumed to lie negligibly close by in sequence space, in statistical terms.
1. New functions are rewarded with a dramatically high selective advantage.
1. Weakened functionality is eliminated with unrealistic efficiency.

Donald Johnson, who holds doctorates in computer science and chemistry, sums up this criticism as follows: AVIDA uses *"an unrealistically small genome, unrealistically high mutation rates, unrealistic protection of the replication instructions, unrealistic energy rewards, and an inability to degrade functionality gradually. This lets the researcher specify arbitrarily the changes that are favourable for selection."*[^19]

The criticism of Avida above should by no means be taken as dismissive; it is analytical[^20]. Taking up simulation research is in itself an excellent thing, despite all the hype around it[^21]. Developing such systems helps to bring evolutionary mechanisms from the level of metaphysics into the domain of testable natural science, and this should be welcomed as a right direction of development that will, it is hoped, continue.

#### Stylus

Clearly, the design-theoretical prediction[^22] about all systems of this kind is that insofar as they seem to work they remain completely unrealistic with respect to biological reality, and insofar as they look reasonably realistic from a biological point of view they turn out not to work.[^23] In other words: according to design theory, many key properties of the objects that biology studies cannot be reached without intelligent purposiveness, and insofar as this claim is true, it will also prove true when the question is approached realistically from the side of simulation.

Design theorists have not left this position resting on mere criticism of Avida and "hand-waving"; they have also built their own simulation software, which aims to match the challenges of biological evolution more realistically and concretely. This software, called *Stylus*, uses a codon coding of graphic vectors of the same kind as the genetic code familiar from biology; the figures it produces must resemble Chinese characters in order to be functional and so gain a selective advantage.
