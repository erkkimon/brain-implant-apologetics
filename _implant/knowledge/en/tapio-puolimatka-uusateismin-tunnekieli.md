# Tapio Puolimatka: The emotional language of New Atheism

*Topics: [Atheism](../topics/ateismi.md), [Tapio Puolimatka](../topics/tapio-puolimatka.md).*

### New Atheism brands its opponents as hogwash

Imagine that, as a professional philosopher, you have analyzed the basic premises of scientific research over more than a thousand pages. You have built your arguments on the research of internationally renowned philosophers such as Charles Taylor, [Alvin Plantinga](../articles/alvin-plantinga.md), and William Alston, as well as on the work of historians of science such as H. Floris Cohen, [Peter Harrison](../articles/peter-harrison.md), and Margaret Osler. Imagine further that a New Atheist association describes the aim of your book in the following way, without justifying the claim with a single reference to the text of the book: "The aim of the book is to throw the premises of all teaching and all science into the rubbish bin, just so that the God of the fundamentalists can be put in His rightful place." What would you reply to this?

The word "fundamentalist" is used in academic discourse to insult and to express contempt. It is an emotional word without precise content. [Alister McGrath](../articles/alister-mcgrath.md) has drawn attention to the fact that New Atheist thinking relies more and more on the power of emotions and images and less and less on arguments: "It is the world of a school debating society, where one resorts to passionate, enthusiastic exaggeration, spiced with glaring oversimplifications and – – misdirection." (Dawkinsin jumala). How could this increase in emotional communication be understood? In my view, behind it may lie atheism's growing intellectual difficulties in three different respects.

First, the naturalist narrative, according to which science advances when belief in God is abandoned, has turned out to be an unhistorical conceptual confusion. Recent research in the history of science shows that the explosive breakthrough of science in the 16th to 18th centuries was based on the basic convictions of Christianity. Its foundation was belief in God, who sustains the law-like processes of nature.

Second, the positivist attempt to exclude faith from science was refuted by analysis in the philosophy of science. The positivist belief in a neutral scientist free of presuppositions turned out to be a myth. Although the intellectual foundation of the myth has crumbled, it still lives in the world of folk beliefs and mass media – it is proclaimed to the public without restraint on the authority of scientists and even philosophers of science.

Third, a turning point has occurred in the philosophical world that may have put atheism on the defensive. According to some of the world's leading epistemologists, atheism is a self-defeating doctrine, because atheism does not make it possible to believe in the reliability of observations and reason.

As the intellectual foundation of atheism crumbles, New Atheism resorts to emotionally appealing images. Charles Taylor analyzes these images in his book *A Secular Age*. When atheism is described as the choice of an intellectually courageous and independent person, people turn to atheism even though its intellectual foundation has collapsed. Evaluating arguments can be troublesome. It is easier to build one's own faith and identity on the dominant world of images.

Calling a worldview one dislikes "huuhaa" (hogwash) is powerful emotional communication. The humorous, childlike emotional language of "huuhaa" makes it possible to discard the opposing worldview without having to present rational arguments to justify one's decision. It is laborious to provide reasoned answers to critics of atheism. It is easier to silence criticism by the power of emotionally appealing images.
