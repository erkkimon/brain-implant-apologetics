# Jesus of Nazareth

*Topics: [Jesus](../topics/jeesus.md).*

Jesus of Nazareth is the central figure of [Christianity](../articles/kristinusko.md). Christians believe that in Jesus the second person of [God](jumala.md) became flesh ("conceived by the [Holy Spirit](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyhä_Henki) <!-- red link: Pyhä Henki -->, born of the virgin Mary") and revealed the [Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_the_Father) <!-- red link: Isä Jumala -->. The name "Jesus" is in [Greek](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/kreikan_kieli) Ίησους (Ieesous), in [Hebrew](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/heprea) יהושע (Jehoshua) or in [Aramaic](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/aramea) ישוע (Jeshua), which means "YHWH saves".[^1]

The real earthly life of Jesus around the beginning of the common era is essential to Christianity, since the meaningfulness of the Christian faith depends heavily on the historicity, in its main features, of the Jesus described in the [New Testament](../articles/uusi-testamentti.md).[^2] The primary sources of information about Jesus' life and teaching are the four accepted gospels,[^3] although scholars argue about the research value of other texts (e.g. the Gospel of Thomas). Jesus is not known to have written anything himself, or at least there is no reason to assume that he did.[^4]

The [reconstruction](../glossary/sanasto.md#rekonstruktio) of Jesus by leading Jesus research, the so-called *historical Jesus*, corresponds surprisingly closely to the picture of him given in Sunday school. News stories in the papers about, for example, Jesus' possible girlfriend do not reflect the [consensus](../glossary/sanasto.md#konsensus) of Jesus research. The problem is that mainstream Jesus research offers nothing newsworthy, whereas news that Jesus was actually gay easily makes the headlines. According to a statement published by Åbo Akademi's historical Jesus research group, "the academic mainstream produces such boring and ordinary results that it cannot compete for reach or visibility", so "bling-bling research comes to be perceived as the consensus".[^5]

### The historicity of Jesus

*Main article: [The historicity of Jesus](jeesuksen-historiallisuus.md)*

Most historians and biblical scholars regard Jesus as a Jewish teacher born in [Galilee](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galilea), who was widely regarded as a healer, was baptized by [John the Baptist](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Kastaja), and was sentenced on the order of [Pontius Pilate](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontius_Pilatus) to death by [crucifixion](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/ristiinnaulitseminen), accused of inciting the people against the Roman Empire.[^6] His death and trial are the most indisputable historical facts about Jesus' life.[^7]

Recently the [Jesus myth hypothesis](jeesus-myyttihypoteesit.md) has surfaced in its various forms, and it seems to convince laypeople far too well considering how probable the historicity of Jesus is. Even among scholars a vanishingly small minority denies the historicity of Jesus, but most of those who deny Jesus are laypeople. The problem is mainly that mainstream research argues for the boring Sunday-school Jesus. That does not make the news, unlike pseudoscientific studies according to which Jesus was actually a woman, gay or a myth.

Most historians and biblical scholars regard Jesus as a Jewish teacher born in Galilee, who was widely regarded as a healer, was baptized by John the Baptist, and was sentenced on the order of Pontius Pilate to death by crucifixion, accused of inciting the people against the Roman Empire.

What about Jesus can we be certain of within the framework of historical research, if we can be certain of anything? First, it is almost clear that Jesus, who was called the Nazarene, existed about 2000 years ago. Jesus' existence is attested by numerous Christian sources and a few non-Christian sources which, given their agenda, would have had no sensible reason to lie that Jesus existed.

The historian Josephus writes about Jesus twice. Josephus' best-known mention of Jesus is called the Testimonium Flavianum. The Testimonium is disputed in that it contains touches that do not seem plausible as the writing of a Jew. The consensus of scholars, however, is that the core of the passage is Josephus' own work. This is suggested by linguistic analysis and by the fact that in Josephus' day Christians had no motive to prove that Jesus existed, only, at most, to prove that an existing person was the Messiah. Usually the authenticity of only three parts of the passage is questioned: the mention of the resurrection, the questioning of his humanity, and the calling of him Christ.

Josephus' second mention of Jesus is in his work Antiquitates Judaicae. It tells of James, whose brother is Jesus who was called Christ. The mention is incidental, so it is hard to interpret it as a forgery. According to the leading Josephus scholar, Louis Feldman, the authenticity of the passage is generally accepted. Moreover, it is extremely unlikely that the passage is about anyone other than the Jesus portrayed in the canonical gospels, since according to it this Jesus was called Christ and was the brother of a man named James.

Jesus is also mentioned by the senator and historian Tacitus. He says that the Christians whom Nero blamed for the fire of Rome took their name from Christ (Christus), "whom the procurator Pontius Pilate had executed in the time of Tiberius" (Annales). Where these details intersect presumably stands the central figure of Christianity, since again it is hard to imagine 1) Pontius Pilate 2) having executed 3) at just that time 4) someone called Christ 5) whom the Christians followed. Moreover, in Latin "christus" was not used as a title the way, say, "procurator" was. Whether or not Tacitus' information was hearsay, he evidently did not doubt that this person existed; nor, apparently, did anyone. Had his existence been in question in his time, there would probably be records of it.

There are further non-Christian written mentions of Jesus, but they leave more room for alternative explanations and conspiracy theories.

##### The gospel accounts in their historical context
Jesus is not known to have left any writings. Instead, the evangelists [Matthew](../books/mat.md), [Mark](../books/mrk.md), [Luke](../articles/evankeliumi-luukkaan-mukaan.md) and [John](../articles/johanneksen-evankeliumi.md), in telling about Jesus, placed him in a particular historical context. The historical context they give does not directly prove the historicity of Jesus, but it strengthens or weakens the authenticity of the accounts. *It is the same kind of thing as when person A tells B that Beijing is the capital of China. B cannot know whether A is telling the truth, but he can check **the details A gives** against other sources and find that A's account is true.*

###### Birth

Matthew and Luke give Jesus' genealogies ( ([Matt. 1:1-17](../books/mat.md)) and  ([Luke 3:23-38](../books/luk.md))). Matthew wanted to prove that Jesus was of David's line. This could be proved before the destruction of the temple in Jerusalem, because the Levites had the task of keeping the genealogies and also of making sure that close relatives did not marry each other. Unfortunately, in AD 70 the Romans destroyed the temple and the genealogies. It can be assumed that before AD 70 Jesus' messianic claim was checked and confirmed as far as his descent was concerned. That the genealogies agree despite their [differences](../articles/joosefin-kaksi-isää.md) supports their authenticity rather than the view that the evangelists made them up.

### Historical reconstruction of his life
