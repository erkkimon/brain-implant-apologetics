# Projective atheistic theory

*Topics: [God](../topics/jumala.md), [Atheism](../topics/ateismi.md).*

The **projective atheistic theory** is one of the best-known defeaters proposed against [theism](teismi.md). At its core is the idea that God does not really exist, but rather that belief in God is the believer's projection of his wishes, imagination or wishful thinking.

### Proponents of the theory

**Philip L. Quinn**[^1] argues that belief in God cannot be a valid [properly basic belief](../glossary/sanasto.md#perususkomus) <!-- red link: perususkomus --> for many [theists](teismi.md), because there are many defeaters for this belief, such as the [argument](../glossary/sanasto.md#argumentti) against the existence of God from the suffering in the world, as well as so-called projective atheistic theories, according to which belief in God is a consequence of people [project](../glossary/sanasto.md#projisoida) for themselves a loving and caring Father God in order to cope in a threatening and cruel world. Of these, the argument from the existence of suffering is a *rebutting defeater*, and the projective theories are *undercutting defeaters*.

The two most famous proponents of projective atheistic theory in Western thought are **[Sigmund Freud](../articles/sigmund-freud.md)** and **[Karl Marx](../articles/karl-marx.md)**. Freud's and Marx's theories are classic examples of the attempt to explain the human need to create for oneself an image of a God who does not actually exist. Both assume that God is a creation of the human mind with no counterpart in reality.

### Criticism of the theory

Freud's and Marx's theories assume that there are no grounds for believing in God. However, they do not present grounds for this claim. They merely express this atheistic conviction vividly and impressively, focusing on explaining how people create various conceptions of God, even though God supposedly does not exist. The validity of these theories rests on the assumption that God does not exist and that humans cannot obtain any other knowledge of God than that He does not exist. However, they do not tell us what this atheistic assumption is based on. They apparently assume that the existence of God depends on humans' ability to prove His existence. In other words, a supposedly omnipotent and omniscient being who has created man and the whole universe can exist only if man, whom He created, wants and is able to construct a proof of His existence that people find indisputable and irrefutable. The fact that Freud's and Marx's writings are able to produce a strong atheistic conviction, even though they do not provide rational grounds against the existence of God, is an interesting fact of the psychology of religion to which sufficient attention has not so far been paid.

Additionally, the projective atheistic theory is a double-edged sword in that it can equally be used to explain atheistic conviction as stemming from secret longings and fear. Atheism can be understood as a religious belief that a person has a need to believe in. For example, the author of *Doctor Zhivago*, **Boris Pasternak** (1890–1960), takes this view of atheism when he says: "I am an atheist who has lost his faith." The belief that there is no God can rest on the hope and desire that this really is so. Atheistic belief can be interpreted as drawing its motivation from a longing for complete self-determination. **Alister McGrath** considers such a possibility:

> It is not difficult to come up with individuals who seem to have an excellent reason to hope and believe that there is no God. Think, for example, of the leader of some liquidation squad during Stalin's purges in the 1930s or a Nazi concentration camp commander during the Second World War. Such a person might experience the thought of God as very threatening. What if God were to regard his actions, the slaughter of great masses of people, as utterly repugnant and decide to punish him for it? Would not such a person have a very good reason to hope that there is no God? Having avoided punishment up to this point, the person most likely hopes that this will happen for all eternity.[^2]

**[Alvin Plantinga](../articles/alvin-plantinga.md)** has dealt thoroughly with Freud's and Marx's arguments in his book [*Warranted Christian Belief*](../articles/warranted-christian-belief-kirja.md), which can be read in full online.
