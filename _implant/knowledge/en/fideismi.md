# Fideism

*Topics: [Philosophy](../topics/filosofia.md).*

**Fideism** is the view in the [philosophy of religion](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uskonnonfilosofia) <!-- red link: uskonnonfilosofia --> that basic religious convictions should not be subjected to the judgment of reason, because religious convictions are not epistemic in nature.

According to fideism, religious beliefs do not need to be subjected to epistemic evaluation, nor do they need to be justified. Thus, fideism differs from both [evidentialism](../articles/evidentialismi.md) and [experientialism](../articles/eksperientialismi.md). According to evidentialism, religious truths should be proven true by means of evidence external to the religion in question. According to experientialism, a person can be convinced of the truths of religion on the basis of immediate experience: for example, if the Bible is a genuine revelation from God, God himself convinces the person of its truth in a way that corresponds, for example, to the immediate conviction, tied to sense perception, that material objects exist outside consciousness. For such epistemic conviction to be possible, the individual would need a sufficiently broad knowledge of the Bible.
