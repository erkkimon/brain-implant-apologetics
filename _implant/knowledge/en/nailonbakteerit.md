# Nylon bacteria

*Topics: [Theory of evolution](../topics/evoluutioteoria.md).*

**Nylon bacteria** are a group of microbes that can break down short nylon 6 polymers (oligomers). Since nylon is a synthetic, non-natural polyamide, the existence of microbes that degrade it has been regarded as an example of the spontaneous emergence of new traits (those required by the theory of evolution).

#### The special nature of nylon?
Regarded as the weightier evidence that nylon-degrading bacteria demonstrate the ability of unguided evolution to create specified complex information (SCI) is the synthetic nature of nylon: it does not arise in nature. Ker Than describes this as follows:

> In 1975, Japanese scientists reported bacteria that could break down nylon, the material used to make pantyhose and parachutes. Bacteria are known to break down all kinds of compounds, from crude oil to sulphur. Thus, the discovery would not have been very significant but for one thing; nylon is synthetic, it did not occur in nature before 1935, when an organic chemist invented it at the Dupont chemical company.
> The discovery of nylon-eating bacteria is a problem for ID proponents. Where did the SCI needed to make nylonase, the protein that breaks down nylon, come from?
> Ker Than
> 23.9.2005
>
> — NBC[^1]

Than proposes three alternatives:
* The nylonase gene already existed, ready-made, in the bacterium's genome.
* A higher being placed the SCI required for nylonase into the bacteria.
* The ability to degrade nylon arose spontaneously through mutation. Because it gave the bacterium the ability to exploit a new resource, the trait was preserved and passed on to future generations.

> Apart from this being the most reasonable explanation, there are also two other reasons why scientists consider the last alternative the best – it is an example of natural selection.
> Ker Than
> 23.9.2005
>
> — NBC[^2]

*Image: Nylon 6, like natural polypeptides, belongs to the polyamides, in which amino acids are joined to each other by peptide bonds (highlighted in yellow). Despite the similarity, nylon 6 is structurally simpler than polypeptides. So it is not surprising that microbes can break it down too.*

#### Biodegradation of nylon

*Image: Three hydrolases that together degrade nylon 6 oligomers: 6-aminohexanoate dimer-cyclo-hydrolase (EI), 6-aminohexanoate hydrolase (EII), and 6-aminohexanoate oligomer hydrolase (EIII)*

*Image: Biodegradation of nylon oligomers. The hydrolysis site is marked with a blue triangle. Hydrolase EI breaks down only the cyclic nylon-6 dimer, EII only straight-chain nylon-6 oligomers, and EIII nylon-6 tetramers. The end product is the nylon-6 monomer, which the bacterium can use as food.*
