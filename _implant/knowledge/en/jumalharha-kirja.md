# The God Delusion (book)

*Topics: [Books](../topics/kirjat.md), [God](../topics/jumala.md), [Atheism](../topics/ateismi.md), [Richard Dawkins](../topics/richard-dawkins.md).*

***The God Delusion*** (Finnish *Jumalharha*; [2006](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006)) is a book by the most prominent preacher of [New Atheism](uusateismi.md), [Richard Dawkins](richard-dawkins.md): a [critique of religion](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/uskontokritiikki) in which Dawkins tries to show that [theism](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/teismi) is [irrational](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/rationaalisuus). In the book Dawkins claims that believing in [God](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/jumala) is irrational and harms [society](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/yhteiskunta). *The God Delusion* is meant as a work of conversion to atheism: "If this book works as I intend, religious readers who open it will be atheists when they put it down."

The book was published in English in 2006 ([Bantam Books](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantam_Books)) and in Finnish, translated by [Kimmo Pietiläinen](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimmo_Pietiläinen), in [2007](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007) ([Terra Cognita](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terra_Cognita)). Pietiläinen claims that this amateurish book on the philosophy of religion is a work of non-fiction, and he is not willing to discuss the subject with those who think otherwise.

### Dawkins's argumentation
#### The central argument
According to Dawkins, the central argument of his book is as follows:
1. One of the greatest challenges to the human intellect has been to explain how complex, improbable, apparently designed things arise.
1. The natural temptation for humans is to take this apparent design for real design.
1. This is a false temptation, because the design hypothesis immediately raises the larger problem of who designed the designer.
1. The most ingenious and powerful explanation is Darwinian evolution by natural selection.
1. We have no equivalent explanation in physics (referring to the [fine-tuning argument](kosminen-hienosäätö.md)).
1. But we should not give up hope that an explanation equivalent to Darwinism will be developed in physics.
1. Therefore God almost certainly does not exist.

But here the conclusion in no way follows from the premises. Moreover, even if the argument were valid, which it is not, all that would follow is that we should not draw a scientific design inference. And even if we draw no design inference, it of course does not follow that we should not believe in God's existence; we have other reasons to believe that God exists.

Furthermore, most of the points in Dawkins's argument are mistaken:

Point 3: Who designed the designer is not a problem. It may be a further scientific question, or simply a bad question. In science one can always ask a further question, say about what an elementary particle is made of, and this does not show that, say, the quark is a bad explanation. If, on the other hand, the designer is God, God is eternal and has no designer. This is because God is a necessary being: He is His own cause and has always existed. 'Who made God?' is therefore as bad a question as 'which way is north from the North Pole?'.

Moreover, Dawkins's demand that an explanation must be simpler than what it explains is not true in general. In archaeology, for example, an arrowhead can be explained by the arrow's maker, who is a more complex explanation, yet still a good and true one. Besides, an almighty God may be a simple explanation. God is Spirit, so He has no parts. Such a mind may have complex thoughts, yet the mind itself may be simple.

Point 4: The Darwinian theory of macroevolution is in reality not a good explanation of complexity. See, for example, [irreducible complexity](../articles/palautumaton-monimutkaisuus.md) and [The Edge of Evolution](the-edge-of-evolution-kirja.md).

Point 5: This is merely an appeal to a future naturalistic explanation. With a working explanation promised for the future, any position can 'prove' anything.

#### Religions and violence
Dawkins also concentrates on the evil deeds done in the name of religions, in practice ignoring both the good deeds of religions (such as the birth of science, universities, hospitals, literacy, tolerance) and the evil deeds and mass murders of atheists, which have killed far more people in a far shorter time (Stalin, Mao Zedong, the Khmer Rouge, [school shootings](../articles/koulumassamurhat.md)).

Dawkins's simplistic view is that if religions were removed, violence and wars would end in the world (or at least decrease considerably). Here Dawkins of course forgets the far greater violence, committed in a far shorter time, in the name of atheism (Stalin, Mao Zedong), and he also fails to mention all the good that has come from religions (science, democracy, education, literacy). But an even bigger problem is that he does not see the real cause of such violence: the evil (fallen) human heart uses various ways of dividing 'us' from 'them'.[^1] So even a peaceful religion can be used to divide. Football hooliganism is one example. There is hardly anything in football itself that incites violence, yet the supporters of different teams sometimes clash quite violently. In fact, in a case such as football hooliganism, what is needed are well-grounded values of peacefulness and tolerance. These values do not arise from atheism but, for example, from Christianity. Atheism gives a violent person a reason to commit exactly the kind of violence he himself wants, because under atheism there is no grounded morality at all. In fact Dawkins himself draws a sharp dividing line between atheism and other religions, abuses those who think differently, makes straw men of their positions and would apparently even be prepared to take their children into care. It seems clear that Dawkins's divisive policy (or one that even forces everyone under atheism) would increase violence by increasing division and intellectual arrogance, and by removing peaceful, violence-reducing worldviews such as Christianity.

#### Misunderstanding religions
Dawkins's definition of religious faith is fideistic. Dawkins defines religious faith as belief that neither needs nor should be justified. Although this definition of faith is common in popular culture, it is not based on evidence; it is a straw man. At least in Christianity, faith has for hundreds of years been held to rest on evidence. This has been the mainstream among theologians, although unfortunately postmodern thinking has nowadays come from society into the church as well, so notable exceptions are starting to appear. In any case, most Christians do not define faith fideistically.

So Dawkins's thinking rests in part on a misunderstanding of religions. Dawkins arbitrarily credits atheism with intellectual virtues and charges religion with intellectual sins such as a lack of critical thinking and of justification. Dawkins's book can indeed be seen as a reaction against religious fideism, the view that religion needs no justification. In fact Christianity, at least, has long traditions of critical thinking (systematic theology, the Reformation), of a balanced relationship between [faith and knowledge](../articles/usko-ja-tieto.md), and of [rational justification](../articles/kristinuskon-perustelut.md). Religions certainly have non-negotiable doctrines too, but so does atheism. The non-negotiable creed and doctrine of atheists is at least that God does not exist. On the other hand, Dawkins seems to think that criticizing other religions is enough to justify his own religion, atheism. But criticizing other positions of course does not prove one's own. Christians certainly claim more, but their position is thereby also more exposed to falsification and to debate in the philosophy of religion, and so it can also be tested more, for example by [historical finds](../articles/raamatun-historiallinen-luotettavuus.md) (we do not expect 100% agreement, because our knowledge is incomplete, but the finds clearly support Christianity in its main outlines). Atheism therefore has fewer intellectual virtues than Christianity.

Dawkins does not seem to admit the fact that every worldview rests partly on faith. Every person believes things that he cannot prove by science. These include moral convictions, belief in the existence of the external world, belief in the existence of other conscious beings, belief in the working of reason (several of which are hard for an atheist to justify under atheism), and belief in the existence or non-existence of God. Moreover, most of what we know we in fact believe by trusting various authorities. Dawkins has himself measured hardly any of the results of science; he relies on the scientific community's testimony about the measurements. Most of history he believes on the strength of the interpretation given by various historians. What matters here is the reliability of the authority. (The Christian holds God's revelation to be reliable, [with good reason](../articles/kristinuskon-perustelut.md).)

#### Dawkins's naive epistemology

What is good in Dawkins's thinking is his emphasis on reasoning based on evidence. Epistemologically, however, this is somewhat naive, because evidence can clearly be interpreted in many ways and from many starting points, which may then substantially affect the conclusions drawn from the evidence. On the other hand, Dawkins does not seem to notice that evidence and justification include areas other than scientific knowledge, which Christian apologetics has traditionally used.

It is ironic that although Dawkins in principle emphasizes reasoning based on evidence, the atheism he promotes is not based on evidence. At best Dawkins would only succeed in showing that God is not needed as an explanation, so that naturalism would be possible. But from naturalism being possible it of course does not follow that naturalism is true. (It is possible that I will win the lottery next week. By Dawkins's method, this proves that I will win the lottery next week.) So Dawkins's chains of reasoning have gaps you could drive a tractor through.
Dawkins calls for evidence, but his own belief is not based on it.

It is also telling that although the results and theories of science are usually uncertain and provisional, Dawkins's atheism is dogmatic, without any doubt whatsoever. This too illustrates the groundless leaps in Dawkins's 'reasoning'.

The fact is that there is not a single measurement or theory in science that would prove atheism. Yet Dawkins implies with his rhetoric that atheism is somehow a position based more on science or empirical evidence.
This rhetoric can be believed thanks to certain modern [myths](../articles/tapio-puolimatka-onko-tiede-kumonnut-jumalan.md) and, on the other hand, because of the fideism of some religious groups, but it has no real foundation. The empirical evidence can be interpreted theistically, as part of God's creation whose beautiful laws God has made, or it can be interpreted atheistically, assuming a closed system. One can even argue that the empirical data point to God. For example, [cosmic fine-tuning](kosminen-hienosäätö.md) is hard to explain under atheism, but the empirical data fit theism well. Likewise, several starting points that science presupposes (the intelligibility of nature, the reliability of human thought, scientific ethics, the existence of laws of nature) fit the Christian world picture better, so the success of science is also circumstantial evidence for Christianity and against atheism.

As we saw above, at best Dawkins's argument would succeed in showing that God is not a necessary explanation of empirical observations (and even in this Dawkins fails). By adding to this a naive argument about the complexity of the God explanation, Dawkins tries to make the God explanation look improbable. Dawkins's only evidence for atheism thus seems to be his claim that God is a complex explanation and therefore improbable. But as we saw above, God is not a complex explanation, and even a complex explanation can be true. So Dawkins has no empirical argument for atheism, nor any philosophical one that withstands scrutiny. Dawkins nevertheless speaks and writes as if atheism were even an empirically justified position, and no doubt many of his Finnish followers, too, imitate this intellectual hypocrisy.

#### Dawkins and morality
Dawkins condemns religiosity as immoral on many levels. The problem is that under atheism there is no objective morality. Under atheism there is no reason why the universe should contain objective moral laws that apply to human beings. In practice Dawkins borrows, somewhat selectively, the Christian moral rules he has learned from our culture. Dawkins's stories about how moral notions arose or developed give no grounds for the rightness of moral rules, nor any motive to follow them, without tacitly assuming some other moral rule or value (such as human dignity). Dawkins represents a new moralism that passes strong moral judgements without justification or even a definition of morality.

#### Freedom of thought

Dawkins fails to mention the violations of freedom of speech and religion, and the state totalitarianism, that have taken place in the name of atheism. Atheism appears to lead to totalitarianism for three reasons:
1. [Intellectual arrogance](../articles/tapio-puolimatka-onko-tiede-kumonnut-jumalan.md), which sees one's own (anti)religious position as the only option for an intelligent person.
1. As atheism spreads, moral decay creates a need to force people who no longer have any intellectual reason to act rightly to act in the desired way.
1. Atheist rulers have no moral reasons to restrain the pursuit of their own interests and their drive for power.

Dawkins continues the line, typical of atheists, of violating religious freedom by claiming that giving a child a religious upbringing (in which Dawkins of course does not count an atheist upbringing) is mental abuse of the child. Behind this apparently lies point 1 of the list above: intellectual arrogance and blindness to one's own faith-based commitment. This totalitarian position of Dawkins's is very worrying, because by his position society should take children from religious homes into care.[^2] What would Dawkins say to someone who proposed that children should be taken into care from parents who represent the religion of atheism? Surely every home's right to bring up its children in its own religion is supported by the strong reason that society cannot take so firm a stand on which religious position is the right one (and in all honesty atheism belongs among them) that it would take the children of the wrong believers into care and program them into the right faith. (Erkki Tuomioja has praised Dawkins's book. It is strange to see a politician supporting such a flagrant violation of religious freedom.)

#### Other arguments

Dawkins also examines various conceptions of God, from the "tyrant of the Old Testament" to the milder variant of certain [Enlightenment](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/valistus) thinkers. Dawkins also deals with the arguments for [the existence of God](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jumalan_olemassaolo) and considers that he shows God to be extremely improbable. According to Dawkins, religion is, among other things, a driving force behind [wars](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sota) <!-- red link: sota -->, fanaticism and child abuse, conveniently forgetting that in communist countries it was precisely in the name of atheism that dissidents were persecuted and children brainwashed into atheist thinking. Dawkins also tries to show Christianity false by pointing to similarities between the Gospels and ancient myths.[^3] Here Dawkins exaggerates the similarities and interprets the concepts of the ancient stories within a Christian framework. Moreover, it can be shown that many of the similarities in the ancient legends are additions made after Christianity arose. And since there are a great many ancient legends and characters in them, it is clear that some similarities will be found when one goes looking for them and interprets the texts to suit.

Many atheists, too, have regretted Dawkins's extreme position and the poor quality of his arguments. He has clearly not familiarized himself with the debate in the philosophy of religion. On the other hand, Dawkins's superficial but rhetorically skilful arguments will surely convince those less familiar with the subject and those who want to believe in the intellectual superiority of their atheism or in the non-existence of God. People believe more in the side whose arguments they hear most.

### Book reviews
The leading philosopher of religion Alvin Plantinga states in his review[^4] that Dawkins's arguments would not be accepted as arguments in essays for an introductory philosophy course.
According to [Helsingin Sanomat](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsingin_Sanomat) book reviewer [Martti-Tapio Kuuskoski](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martti-Tapio_Kuuskoski), the book encourages atheists to come out of the closet. According to the reviewer, the book also gives moderate believers a roasting, though it will hardly put an end to religions.[^5] According to [Aamulehti](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aamulehti) book reviewer [Vesa Vanhalakka](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesa_Vanhalakka) <!-- red link: Vesa Vanhalakka -->, Dawkins speaks bluntly and goes on a crusade against all religions. According to the reviewer, Dawkins proclaims the good news of atheism. He judges that Dawkins's hope of believers converting to atheism will remain a dream.[^6]

### Lawsuit

Erol Kraaslan, the publisher of the Turkish edition of *The God Delusion*, was charged in 2007 with "insulting believers" after selling 6,000 copies of the book. One reader filed a complaint and demanded that the book be withdrawn from sale. Erol Kraaslan could have received a prison sentence of up to six months.[^7]

In the United States a young man committed suicide after reading Dawkins's book.[^8] When the boy's grieving father raised the matter, he received large amounts of hate mail from atheists.
