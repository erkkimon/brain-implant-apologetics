# The goodness of God

*Topics: [God](../topics/jumala.md), [Ethics](../topics/etiikka.md).*

According to the Bible, only [God](jumala.md) is [good](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/hyvyys).[^1] The goodness of God has often been called into question, but God's goodness has nothing to do with God's [existence](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/eksistenssi).[^2]

### The goodness of God described through imaginary examples

Take Pertti, who thinks that God is not good. What actually is goodness? Can Pertti say? Goodness also depends on one's point of view and time scale. No human being sees the whole history of the world at once. Let us further suppose that God does not even try to be good. He merely claims to be so, and is at heart a twisted sadist and despot. He has nevertheless created the world and everything in it. He decides what is done. He has created the laws of nature, all the planets and living things. He has made the rules. Suppose he has said:

> If you are not wildly enthusiastic about ending up in hell, then do squat jumps on your right ear a few billion times and then hang yourself with your own intestines.

If one wants to go to heaven, there is not really any alternative to obeying. God is [omnipotent](jumalan-kaikkivoipuus.md), and the omnipotent one decides what is right. So from a philosophical point of view he is just in any case, since he decides what justice is.

The claim can be illustrated with an example. Imagine a programmer who writes a fun game. The game has 100 characters, of which 13 are chosen at random for bit heaven and the rest for bit hell. 13 get into bit heaven, and that is that. The programmer made the rules, and the complaints of the 87 characters who end up in bit hell about the programmer's wickedness or injustice make no difference.

#### Conclusion

God is good if we understand, or were to understand, his perfect logic. The situation does not change even if God were unjust or evil, since the rules He has laid down would still apply. Whoever experiences God as good and as wanting what is best for him obeys God gladly.

### A good or an evil deed?

Mauri is a kind, demented and deaf old man whose hobby is going all over town on his rollator. Mauri is being watched by big, hairy Martti, who shadows him and follows his every move. Martti has a double-barrelled shotgun. Mauri finds a good hill and starts picking up speed. Mauri revels in the rush of speed. Martti starts to fall behind and can no longer keep up on foot. He stops, loads, aims and shoots off Mauri's left leg, and then shoots off the remaining right leg as well. Mauri falls off and curses heavily. We can of course say that Martti acted stupidly and wrongly. It is not nice to shoot someone's legs off.

Let us, however, continue the story. The rollator rolls on to an intersection, where a lorry runs over it and it is completely destroyed. After that a second lorry runs over it too. So Martti actually saved Mauri's life by knocking him off the rollator, since he had no way of communicating with the deaf Mauri. Nor could he catch up with Mauri. From this point of view, is shooting off the legs an evil or a good deed? For now it would seem that the deed was good. Perhaps Mauri was a believer at the moment of the shooting and would have gone to heaven, but after the shooting incident he, for one reason or another, gave up his faith and ended up in hell. Perhaps the deed caused only evil after all? This is impossible to know. Everything depends on the final consequences of events, which will be known only at the very end, and that may still be thousands of years away. For this reason, among others, it is rather difficult for us to set about judging what is good and what is evil. That is why only the eternal God can know.
