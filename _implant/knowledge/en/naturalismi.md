# Naturalism

*Topics: [Worldview](../topics/maailmankatsomus.md).*

Naturalism is an ambiguous concept that can mean many different things.

### The naturalistic research program

Naturalism can be understood as a [research program](../articles/tutkimusohjelma.md). According to the [naturalistic research program](../articles/naturalistinen-tutkimusohjelma.md), the reliable sources of knowledge are observations that can be publicly checked, together with the principles of rational inference and theory choice. The alternatives to naturalism in this sense are the intuitionist and the [supernaturalist research program](../articles/supranaturalistinen-tutkimusohjelma.md).

[Michael C. Rea](../articles/michael-c-rea.md) understands naturalism in this way and argues in his book [World Without Design](../articles/world-without-design-kirja.md) that a naturalist cannot, in an epistemically justified way, believe in the existence of objects outside consciousness or of other conscious beings.

### The naturalistic worldview

Naturalism can also be understood as a comprehensive worldview according to which God or any godlike being does not exist. According to naturalism, everything that exists, human beings included, consists solely of natural constituents and can be studied scientifically, and there is nothing supernatural. All events must be explained in terms of natural causes. Naturalists usually regard the scientific method as the only reliable way to gain knowledge of what exists. In their view, the use of the scientific method supports the view that the constituents of reality are either physical or causally dependent on physical objects and their modifications. The opposite is supernaturalism, according to which God is a purely spiritual being, causally independent of physical objects.

[Alvin Plantinga](../articles/alvin-plantinga.md) mostly understands naturalism in this sense and argues that the naturalist has a defeater that cannot be defeated. From a naturalistic starting point it is not possible to justify trust in the reliability of reason and perception. Since the naturalist forms his own naturalistic conviction by relying on reason and perception, he has no reason to believe that naturalism offers a true picture of reality.

### Methodological naturalism

Naturalism can also be understood as a methodological principle, [methodological naturalism](metodologinen-naturalismi.md) or methodological atheism, according to which all events must be explained in terms of natural causes. A methodological approach that competes with methodological naturalism is dialogical pluralism, according to which, in scientific research, each researcher must openly state their ultimate starting points. The epistemic justification of these starting points can then be compared on the basis of their explanatory power.
