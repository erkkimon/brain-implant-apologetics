# The conditions of evolution

*Topics: [Theory of evolution](../topics/evoluutioteoria.md), [Incomplete articles](../topics/keskeneräiset-artikkelit.md).*

**[Petter Portin](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petter_Portin)**, emeritus professor of genetics at the University of Turku, has set out his view on the necessary and sufficient conditions of evolution in an [article](http://journal.fi/tt/article/view/56864) published in the journal *Tieteessä tapahtuu*:

"This article sets out the necessary conditions of biological evolution on the one hand and of cultural development on the other, insofar as they are known. Together, the necessary conditions of a phenomenon form its sufficient condition. All three necessary conditions of biological evolution are known – – The theory of biological evolution can nowadays be regarded as complete in its principles, although there is not yet full agreement on how the mechanism of macroevolution, that is, the origin of species, is derived from evolution within a species, that is, microevolution. – – In any case, the necessary conditions of biological evolution, which together form its sufficient condition, are known (Lewontin 1982) and they have all been proven both theoretically and empirically in the most varied ways. – – All necessary conditions of biological evolution, which together form its sufficient condition (Lewontin 1982, p. 149), are: the principle of variation, the principle of heredity, and the principle of selection. These principles can be called the basic propositions or basic premises, that is, postulates, of the theory of evolution." (p. 7).

In itself, it is wholly positive that the logical foundations of the theory of evolution have thus been laid open "to the general public". But what light, when critically analysed, does the perspective that Prof. Portin has opened, following **[Richard Lewontin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Lewontin)**, actually shed on the content and credibility of the evolutionary model?
