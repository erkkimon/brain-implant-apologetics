# God's omnipotence

*Topics: [God](../topics/jumala.md).*

[Christian](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/kristinusko) [theism](teismi.md) generally holds that omnipotence is one of [God's](jumala.md) [attributes](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/ominaisuus). Omnipotence, or being all-powerful, means authority and power without limits. In the [1933/38 Finnish Bible translation](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanha_kirkkoraamattu), God is called almighty in 59 verses.[^1]

> One can ask: *can God create a stone so large that he cannot lift it?*
> #If God can create such a stone, he cannot lift it, and so he is not omnipotent.
> #If God cannot create such a stone, he is not able to do just anything after all, and so he is not omnipotent.
> #An omnipotent being does not stand up to logical scrutiny.

The claim can also be put in a general form: "Can God do something that he cannot do?"

[Logical](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logiikka) scrutiny is indeed the key word here. If we keep to the rules of logic, we notice that an omnipotent being can lift every object, so the objects he cannot lift form, in [set-theoretic](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/joukko-oppi) terms, the [empty set](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/tyhjä_joukko). We can illustrate this with the following question:

> *Can God create a square circle?*

The question is of course meaningless in itself, since square circles are a logical impossibility. As C. S. Lewis puts it in his book *Kärsimyksen ongelma* (*The Problem of Pain*):

> Nonsense remains nonsense even when we try to attach it to God.
>
> — C. S. Lewis

If we want to examine whether omnipotence is a logically possible attribute, the very framing of the task already assumes that the omnipotent being acts logically. An omnipotent being bound by logic can do everything that is logically possible, so he does not need to be able to do logical impossibilities.

One can of course toy with the idea that an omnipotent being would not need to be bound by the rules of logic, but then it would presumably be unreasonable to demand that the omnipotent being stand up to logical scrutiny.

On this basis one can, for example, claim that God is able to do things that are [impossible](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/mahdottomuus) for him, for the simple reason that he can do anything. A human being does not need to understand how this happens, or how it would be logically possible, because it does not need to be logically possible.

We can also think that God, even within the rules of logic, is able to create a stone that he cannot lift. This, however, requires that he limit his omnipotence with respect to this stone, so that once he has created the stone he would no longer be omnipotent.

#### Can God lie?

It is often claimed that God could not change or lie. If God is omnipotent, this means that God can do anything. If God could not lie or change, he would not fit the definition of omnipotent.

> The chain of reasoning can be set out as follows:
> # God is omnipotent.
> # An omnipotent God can do anything.
> # God cannot lie.
> # There is an [a priori](../glossary/sanasto.md#a-priori) contradiction between points 2 and 3.

So we can conclude that an omnipotent God 1) can change and 2) can lie. He does not, however, necessarily do so. If we assume that God is an omnipotent [agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agency_(philosophy)), he could create for every human being a personal [football](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalkapallo) hovering half a metre above their head. He is, however, under no compulsion whatsoever to do so. Although Martti could in theory kill Mauri at any moment, he does not necessarily do it. Not everything that can be done has to be done. This is called free will.
