# Cambrian explosion

*Topics: [Theory of evolution](../topics/evoluutioteoria.md).*


In strata about 570 million years old, a huge number of different fossils seems to appear as if out of nowhere. Depending on how the various species and groups of species are defined, some 30-40 phyla seem to have arisen within what is, geologically speaking, a very short time.[^1] Even fossils of vertebrate animals have been found in these Cambrian strata.[^2]

### Duration

The Cambrian explosion happened quite quickly. Estimates of its exact duration vary, but the consensus of researchers probably holds this phase to have lasted at most about 50 million years.[^3] Michael Behe, however, states that recently the estimated duration of the period has shrunk from 50 million years to 10 million.[^4] How short the period is in terms of evolutionary events is perhaps illustrated by the fact that the common ancestor of humans and chimpanzees is estimated to have lived 5-10 million years ago. So the speed of evolution in the Cambrian must truly have been explosive. The world-famous Harvard University palaeontologist Stephen J. Gould has proposed that the rapid rate at which new species appeared would require some mechanism other than natural selection.[^5] The problem was already familiar to Darwin himself, who stated: "To the question why we do not find a rich fossil record in strata older than the Cambrian, I can give no satisfactory answer."[^6]

### Precambrian fossils - no predecessors?

Judging from the fossil record, the Precambrian, the period before the Cambrian, was quite poor in life. No ancestors of the species found in the Cambrian strata have been found in the older Precambrian strata. For a long time it was assumed that the Precambrian strata contain no multicellular animal fossils at all. Many of the claimed fossil finds from these strata turn out to be so-called pseudofossils, that is, inorganic formations that resemble a fossilised organism. Preston Cloud, for example, stated in the journal Geology in 1973 that not a single undisputed multicellular organism had been found in strata older than the Cambrian.[^7] Since then, genuine fossils have also been found in Precambrian strata.[^8] Despite the finds, the Cambrian explosion remains, in the words of the famous evolutionist G. G. Simpson, one of the "major mysteries of the history of life".[^9]

### What does it mean?

In the view of the famous evolutionist Simpson, 75% of evolution had already taken place by the time the Cambrian arrived. By this he refers to the fact that the huge development from a primitive single-celled organism to thousands of complex aquatic animals had already happened by the start of the Cambrian. Although the figure, which Simpson evidently meant as a rough estimate, should not be taken too literally, it does help show that the Cambrian involves problems. If, within that 75%, no ancestors and lines of development are found for the Cambrian animals, how do we know that this development happened at all? Luther Sunderland remarks sarcastically: "In other words, today evolution proceeds so slowly that it is not observed, but in the past it happened at such a speed that it left no trace at all in the fossil record."[^10] Discussion of the theory of evolution and of the evidence of the fossil record often centres on Archaeopteryx, the evolution of the whale or the horse series, but in the supposed development these are really only the tip of the iceberg. Most of the development is considered to have happened precisely in the time before the Cambrian, because all the phyla of the animal kingdom were already in place 500 million years ago.[^11] So the strata before the Cambrian should contain a huge number of transitional fossils, which, however, have not been found. As Philip Johnson states: "The greatest problem the fossil record poses for Darwinism is the Cambrian explosion. Almost all the phyla of the animal kingdom appear in the rocks of this period without a trace of the ancestors the Darwinists require."[^12]

### Evolutionists' explanations

Perhaps the most significant anti-creationist site on the Internet is Talk.Origins, which takes an active part in the furious battle being fought on the net. One of the talkorigins activists, Chris Colby, refers to the Cambrian explosion in his article "Introduction to Evolutionary Biology"[^13] and plays down its significance. According to him, this "explosion", despite its speed, was not instantaneous on the geological timescale. As the reason for the rapid appearance of the animals, Colby suggests either a rise in oxygen levels or the spread of shallow seas at just that time, which would have given new forms suitable isolated places to develop. When we take into account the evolutionary mechanism and the limits that time sets on it, we have to state, against Colby, that the explosion really was, in practice, "instantaneous". Although Colby's text, being general in nature, cannot be expected to dig deep into the problems of the Cambrian explosion, playing the problem down does not convince those who know the subject. Researchers have offered the following kinds of explanations for the Cambrian explosion and for the scarcity of fossils in the strata before it.

1. The amount of oxygen in the sea rose so strongly that a huge leap in the evolutionary process followed. This explanation is lame at least because oxygen levels seem to have been quite high for at least two billion years.[^14] This theory does not answer the real problem, the speed of development. How was an evolutionary mechanism based on random mutations and natural selection able to "create" so many forms of life in a short time? With oxygen or without.
1. Because the ancestors of the Cambrian animals were soft-bodied, they did not leave fossils to the same extent as hard-bodied animals. Fossils of soft-bodied animals have, however, been found, for example in the Burgess fauna in Canada, which in time lies relatively close to the Precambrian. It is also to be expected that the so-called transitional forms would have had to be at least partly of "hard" material, if they were transitional forms at all. The American law professor Philip Johnson, one of the best-known opponents of Darwinism in the world at present, wonders why Nils Eldredge, who specialises in marine invertebrates, uses as evidence for evolution not marine animals but human evolution.[^15] Apparently convincing examples of transitional fossils are simply not to be found in that group.[^16] This is strange, given that fossils form most of all precisely in aquatic conditions, as the book under discussion also points out.
1. The Precambrian strata were eroded away, and with them all the ancestors that preceded the Cambrian forms of life. This (Laplalian interval) theory is today a generally rejected explanation.[^17]
1. Professor James Valentine of the University of California, Berkeley has proposed that primitive organisms accumulated enough development-regulating hox genes, and that this made rapid development possible.[^18] Valentine's proposal has been criticised by, among others, the biologist Jonathan Wells, according to whom hox genes are only "start buttons" that produce no new information.[^19]

### Conclusion

The Cambrian explosion remains, at least for now, a mystery and a thorn in the flesh of the present theory of evolution. Where did a large number of different forms of life suddenly appear from into the fossil strata? Are we studying only the last kilometre of the marathon because it was run in the stadium, and waving away any look at the many miles that came before that lap?
